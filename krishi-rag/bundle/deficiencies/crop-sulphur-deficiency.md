# Visual symptoms

- **Uniform pale yellowing of the YOUNGEST leaves at the top of the plant**,
  while older lower leaves stay green.
- Whole leaf pales evenly — not patches, not spots, not stripes between veins.
- Stems may turn reddish in mustard.
- Plants stunted with thin stems; in oilseeds, poor pod filling and lower oil
  content.

# Distinguishing from similar conditions

The top-versus-bottom rule is the whole diagnosis, and it is reliable because
it follows from how the nutrients move inside the plant.

| Looks like | Tell it apart by |
|---|---|
| **Nitrogen deficiency** | Nitrogen moves within the plant, so it is pulled out of **old** leaves to feed new growth — the **bottom** leaves yellow first. Sulphur does not move, so the **top** leaves yellow first. This single check separates the two. |
| Iron deficiency | Iron also hits young leaves, but yellows **between the veins while the veins stay green**. Sulphur yellows the whole leaf evenly. |
| Yellow mosaic virus | Virus gives **mottled patches of yellow and green on the same leaf**, often puckered, and spreads plant to plant. Sulphur is even and does not spread. |

# Critical treatment note

**This is not a disease.** No fungicide or insecticide affects it, and an
insecticide sprayed for suspected virus vectors is money wasted on a soil
problem.

**This device does not carry verified sulphur doses.** Direct the farmer to a
Krishi Vigyan Kendra with a soil test.

# Where this is common

Increasingly widespread as farmers move to high-analysis fertilisers such as
urea and DAP, which supply no sulphur, unlike older materials like single super
phosphate and ammonium sulphate. Oilseeds and pulses have a high sulphur demand.
Research from Birsa Agricultural University documents sulphur response in
soybean on Ranchi's acidic upland soils.
