**What the pre-harvest interval is**
The pre-harvest interval, also called the waiting period, is the number of days that must pass between the last pesticide spray and harvest.
It is printed on the pesticide label for each crop.
Counting those days lets the residue break down to a safe level before the produce is picked.

**What a Maximum Residue Limit is**
The Maximum Residue Limit, or MRL, is the highest amount of a pesticide residue legally allowed on a food commodity.
MRLs in India are notified by the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India under the Food Safety and Standards Regulations.
The limit is worked out from supervised field trials, where the pesticide is applied at the approved dose and the residue is measured over time.

**Why the waiting period matters to the farmer's income**
- Produce within the MRL passes food safety testing and moves freely in the mandi and in export channels.
- Buyers, exporters and organised retail chains test consignments. A clean residue report keeps the buyer coming back.
- Consignments meeting residue norms qualify for export and fetch a better price.
- Following the label dose and the waiting period is what Good Agricultural Practice means in official terms.

**How to get it right**
1. Read the waiting period from the label before spraying.
2. Write the spray date in a field diary or on the calendar in the house.
3. Count forward from that date to the earliest harvest date.
4. Harvest on or after that date.
5. Plan sprays backwards from the expected picking date, especially for vegetables and fruit that are picked repeatedly.

**Crops that need extra attention**
Vegetables and fruits harvested every few days — bhindi, brinjal, chilli, tomato, cucurbits, grapes, beans — carry the highest residue risk, because the picking interval can be shorter than the waiting period.
For these, choose products with a short waiting period, prefer bio-pesticides and neem formulations close to harvest, and stagger picking blocks so a sprayed block is left to complete its waiting period.

⚠ The waiting period differs for every pesticide and every crop. Take it from the label of the actual product in hand.
