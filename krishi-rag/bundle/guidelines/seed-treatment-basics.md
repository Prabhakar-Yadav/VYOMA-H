**Why treat seed**
Seed treatment protects the seed and the young seedling from soil-borne and seed-borne disease and from early insect attack.
It is the cheapest plant protection step in the whole season, since a small quantity of material protects the whole crop at its most vulnerable stage.
It also improves germination and gives an even, vigorous stand.

**The correct order — three steps, in this sequence**

1. **Fungicide first.**
   Treat with the recommended seed dressing fungicide for that crop.
   This handles seed-borne fungal diseases, smuts, seedling blight and damping off.

2. **Insecticide second.**
   Treat with the recommended seed treatment insecticide where early sucking pest or termite attack is expected.

3. **Biofertiliser and bio-agent last, just before sowing.**
   Treat with Rhizobium for pulses and groundnut, Azospirillum or Azotobacter for cereals, and phosphate solubilising bacteria for most crops.
   Bio-agents such as Trichoderma and Pseudomonas also go at this stage.

Keep a gap between each step, and dry the seed in the shade after each one.
Biological cultures go on last and go on close to sowing, because live organisms survive better when they meet the soil soon.

**Where a bio-agent is the whole treatment**
Where the farmer chooses a fully biological route, Trichoderma or Pseudomonas alone is used as the seed treatment. This is the standard route for organic and natural farming, and it keeps the seed compatible with the Rhizobium culture that follows.

**How to treat the seed — the practical method**

1. Work in the shade, on a clean cloth or polythene sheet or in a clean drum.
2. Wear gloves and a mask.
3. Measure the seed. Measure the treatment material per the label rate for that weight of seed.
4. Sprinkle a little clean water or a sticker such as rice gruel, jaggery solution or gum, so the material holds on the seed.
5. Add the treatment material and mix until every seed carries an even coat. A hand-operated seed treating drum does this best.
6. Spread the seed in the shade and let it dry.
7. Sow the same day where the treatment is biological. Fungicide-treated seed can be held a short while as the label allows.

**Safety points**
- Treat seed away from the kitchen, food grain and drinking water.
- Wear gloves, a mask and full clothing. Wash hands and face with soap afterwards.
- Keep treated seed clearly marked and out of reach of children and animals.
- Treated seed is planting material only. Keep it entirely separate from grain meant for eating or for feed.
- Sow all the treated seed. Bury any leftover treated seed deep in a waste area.
- Empty treatment packets are crushed and buried, like pesticide containers.

⚠ Products, rates and compatibility differ by crop and by state recommendation. Take the current recommendation from the KVK, the state agriculture department package of practices, or the Kisan Call Centre.
