**The official position**
Government guidance is to keep crop residue in the field or put it to use, rather than burn it.
Burning destroys the organic carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium and sulphur held in the straw. It kills soil microbes and earthworms in the top layer. It adds to air pollution.
The Crop Residue Management scheme supports both in-situ and ex-situ management with machinery subsidy and project support.

**Route 1 — In-situ management. Keep the residue in the field.**

Machines supported under the scheme:
- **Super Straw Management System (Super SMS)** fitted to the combine harvester. It chops and spreads the straw evenly while harvesting, instead of leaving a windrow.
- **Happy Seeder.** Sows wheat directly into standing paddy stubble, cutting and lifting the straw and dropping it back as mulch over the sown row.
- **Super Seeder.** Cuts, lifts and mixes the straw into the soil and sows in one pass.
- **Smart Seeder** and **Surface Seeder.** Lighter options for sowing into residue.
- **Zero Till Seed cum Fertiliser Drill.** Sows into the untilled field.
- **Mulcher, Shredder and Rotary Slasher.** Chop the residue fine and spread it.
- **Reversible Mould Board Plough.** Turns residue into the soil.
- **Crop Reaper and Reaper Binder.** Cuts and bundles the straw for removal.

What in-situ management gives back:
- Organic carbon builds in the soil year after year.
- The nutrients held in the straw return to the field.
- Mulch holds soil moisture and cuts the first irrigation requirement.
- Weed pressure falls under a straw mulch.
- Soil temperature stays steadier for the young crop.
- Sowing the next crop happens sooner, since the field needs no clearing.

**Route 2 — Ex-situ management. Take the residue out and use it.**

Collection machines: Baler and Straw Rake. These gather the loose straw into bales that can be lifted and transported.

Uses for collected straw:
- Fuel pellets and briquettes for thermal power plants and industrial boilers
- Compressed Bio-Gas (CBG) and bio-CNG plants
- Biogas
- Ethanol and alcohol production
- Cattle fodder, after treatment
- Composting into farmyard-quality manure
- Mushroom growing substrate
- Packaging board, paper and biodegradable products
- Farm bedding

The revised guidelines allow techno-commercial pilot projects where a farmer or FPO signs a bilateral agreement with an industry that will take the straw, and biomass collection depots are set up to aggregate it.

**Financial support**
- 50 percent of machine cost to an individual farmer for crop residue management machinery.
- 80 percent of project cost for projects up to Rupees 30 lakh, for rural entrepreneurs, cooperative societies, registered farmer societies, FPOs and panchayats setting up a Custom Hiring Centre.
- A farmer who does not want to buy can hire the machine from a Custom Hiring Centre. Find nearby machines on the FARMS app or through the CHC portal.

**Practical sequence for a paddy-wheat farmer**
1. Fit or hire a combine harvester with a Super SMS attached, so the straw comes out chopped and spread.
2. Choose the sowing route — Happy Seeder or Super Seeder to sow wheat directly into the residue.
3. Book the machine from the Custom Hiring Centre before the harvest window opens, since demand peaks in a narrow period.
4. Where the straw is to be removed, book a baler and arrange the buyer or depot in advance.
5. Give the first irrigation a little later than usual, since the mulch holds moisture.

⚠ The Crop Residue Management scheme is focused on Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and NCT of Delhi. Other states support the same machinery through SMAM and the state agriculture department, under different names and rates.
