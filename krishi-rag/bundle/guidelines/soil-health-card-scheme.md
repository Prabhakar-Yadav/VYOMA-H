**What the farmer gets**
A printed card showing the nutrient status of the farmer's own soil.
The card carries crop-wise fertiliser recommendations and soil amendment advice.
Testing and the card are provided free to the farmer.

The card reports 12 parameters:
- Main nutrients — Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potassium (K)
- Secondary nutrient — Sulphur (S)
- Micronutrients — Zinc (Zn), Iron (Fe), Copper (Cu), Manganese (Mn), Boron (B)
- Physical parameters — pH, Electrical Conductivity (EC), Organic Carbon (OC)

**Who qualifies**
Every farmer holding cultivable land qualifies. There is no landholding limit and no income limit.

**How to get soil tested**
1. Approach the village level soil testing lab, the Krishi Vigyan Kendra, the block agriculture office or the district soil testing laboratory.
2. Agriculture department staff or a trained village youth collect the sample with a GPS-tagged location.
3. Samples are collected on a grid — one sample for every 2.5 hectares in irrigated land, and one for every 10 hectares in rainfed land.
4. Sampling is done after harvest, when the field carries no standing crop. This usually happens twice a year, after Rabi harvest and after Kharif harvest.
5. The lab tests the sample and uploads results to the national Soil Health Card portal.
6. The card is handed to the farmer and is also available to download from soilhealth.dac.gov.in using the mobile number or the card number.

A farmer may also carry a properly drawn sample directly to the nearest soil testing lab or KVK.

**Documents needed**
- Aadhaar or another identity proof
- Land record or survey number
- Mobile number for the card to be linked and traced

**Deadline pattern**
Sampling runs in campaign mode after each harvest.
⚠ The scheme now runs as the Soil Health and Fertility component under the umbrella Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana. Village level soil testing labs and mobile testing vans are being expanded, so the way a farmer books a test differs from state to state.
