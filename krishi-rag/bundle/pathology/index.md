# Plant pathology

## The single most valuable thing in this corpus

**Which kind of organism causes the disease, and why that decides what works.**

A fungicide acts on fungi. Bacterial and viral diseases are managed by cultural
practice, clean planting material and resistant varieties. A farmer who does not
know that difference buys a fungicide for bacterial leaf blight, sprays it, and
loses both the money and the crop.

That is a recurring, expensive, entirely preventable mistake — and it is exactly
the kind of expert knowledge the device exists to carry.

## What belongs here

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Organism type — fungus, oomycete, bacterium, virus, phytoplasma, nematode | Decides the entire management approach |
| Disease cycle | Where it survives, how it reaches the plant, how it enters |
| Weather triggers | Temperature, humidity and leaf-wetness ranges that start an epidemic |
| How it spreads | Rain splash, wind, insect vector, seed, soil, irrigation water, tools |
| Seed-borne or soil-borne | Decides whether the fix is seed selection or land management |
| Soil survival period | Tells a farmer how long a field stays unsafe for that crop |
| Why resistance breaks down | Sets honest expectations about resistant varieties |
| Mode-of-action rotation | Why repeated use of one chemical group stops working |

## Relationship to `diseases/`

`diseases/` answers *what does this look like* — symptom text written the way a
vision model describes a photograph, used for identification.

`pathology/` answers *what is actually happening and why* — the biology behind
the symptom. Both are needed, and both are reached by the same search.

## Writing rule

**State what is present, never what is absent.** Write "fungicides act on fungi;
bacterial diseases are managed by cultural control and resistant varieties" —
not "fungicides do not work on bacteria." Negation makes the text match the very
thing it tries to exclude.
