# PART 2 — Major Pests & Diseases of the Top 20 Crops

**Urgency scale used:**
- 🔴 **Emergency (days)** — can destroy a crop or field patch in under ~1 week
- 🟠 **Fast (1–2 weeks)** — serious loss if not acted on within the fortnight
- 🟡 **Moderate (weeks)** — progresses over the season
- 🔵 **Slow / chronic** — no in-season rescue; management is for next season

---

## 2.1 RICE (धान)

| Problem | Scientific name | Local name | Type | Urgency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rice blast** | *Pyricularia oryzae* (teleomorph *Magnaporthe oryzae*) | झुलसा रोग *jhulsa rog*; Ta *kuruthu noi* | Fungal | 🟠→🔴 at neck stage |

**Visual:** Diamond / spindle / eye-shaped lesions on the leaf blade, 0.5–1.5 cm long, **pointed at both ends**, with an **ash-grey or whitish centre and a distinct brown-to-reddish-brown border**, often with a thin yellow halo. Lesions merge into large irregular burnt patches. *Neck blast:* a **blackish-brown girdling band right at the base of the panicle**; the panicle snaps and hangs over, grains empty and chaffy (white heads). *Nodal blast:* black band at a stem node, stem breaks there.

| **Bacterial leaf blight (BLB)** | *Xanthomonas oryzae* pv. *oryzae* | जीवाणु झुलसा *jeevanu jhulsa* | Bacterial | 🟠 |

**Visual:** Drying starts at the **leaf TIP and along the MARGINS** and moves downward. The boundary between the yellow-straw dead tissue and the green tissue is **wavy / undulating** — this is the single best diagnostic. Early lesions are water-soaked and yellowish. In the early morning, **milky-yellow bead-like bacterial ooze droplets** sit on cut leaf ends. Severe form (*kresek*) in seedlings: whole plant wilts and dies, looks like drought damage. Worsens sharply after storms and flooding.

| **Sheath blight** | *Rhizoctonia solani* AG1-IA | "snake-skin disease" | Fungal | 🟡→🟠 |

**Visual:** Starts on the **leaf sheath near the water line**. Oval, greenish-grey, water-soaked lesions that enlarge into irregular blotches with a **straw/grey-white centre and an irregular dark brown wavy border** — the mottled pattern is described as **snake-skin**. Look for **white cottony mycelial threads** on the sheath and small, **mustard-seed-sized round sclerotia** that start white and turn brown-black and fall off easily. Climbs up the plant in a dense canopy.

| **Brown spot** | *Bipolaris oryzae* (*Cochliobolus miyabeanus*) | भूरा धब्बा *bhura dhabba* | Fungal (+ soil nutrient stress) | 🟡 |

**Visual:** Many **small, evenly scattered, oval-to-circular brown spots** over the whole leaf — often described as sesame-seed-shaped. Mature spots have a **grey or tan centre with a reddish-brown margin**. Distinguish from blast: brown spot is **round/oval and scattered**, blast is **spindle-shaped with pointed ends**. Strongly linked to poor, sandy, potassium/silicon-deficient soils. Also causes black speckling on grains.

| **Yellow stem borer** | *Scirpophaga incertulas* | तना छेदक *tana chhedak* | Insect | 🟡 |

**Visual:** *Vegetative stage* — **"dead heart"**: the central whorl leaf turns yellow-brown and dries while the outer leaves stay green; it **pulls out easily with a gentle tug**. *Reproductive stage* — **"white ear head"**: the entire panicle is white/silvery, stands erect (because empty), grains unfilled, and the tiller pulls out easily. Look for a small round entry hole on the stem and a creamy-white caterpillar inside when split open.

| **Brown planthopper (BPH)** | *Nilaparvata lugens* | भूरा फुदका *bhura fudka*; Te *sudi purugu* | Insect | 🔴 |

**Visual:** **Circular patches of the field turn yellow → orange-brown → completely dry and lodge**; the patch expands outward in rings ("hopper burn"). Part the canopy and look at the **base of the tillers just above the water** — dense clusters of small brown wedge-shaped hoppers and white nymphs. Sticky honeydew and **black sooty mould** coat the lower stems. A hopper-burn patch can collapse in under a week.

| **Rice leaf folder** | *Cnaphalocrocis medinalis* | पत्ती लपेटक *patti lapetak* | Insect | 🟡 |

**Visual:** Leaves **folded lengthwise into a tube and stitched with silk**. Inside, the caterpillar scrapes the green layer off, leaving **white/transparent papery longitudinal streaks**. From a distance the crop looks whitish-streaked.

| **False smut** | *Ustilaginoidea virens* | लक्ष्मी रोग *Lakshmi rog* (some farmers treat it as auspicious) | Fungal | 🔵 |

**Visual:** Scattered individual grains in the panicle replaced by **round velvety spore balls, larger than a normal grain**, starting orange-yellow → olive-green → dull black, with a powdery surface that leaves colour on the fingers.

| **Rice tungro** | Rice tungro bacilliform + spherical virus; vector green leafhopper *Nephotettix virescens* | टुंग्रो | Viral | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Yellow-to-orange discolouration starting at the tip of the younger leaves** and spreading down, with rusty specks; plants stunted with reduced tillering; damage appears in **irregular patches**, not uniform. Distinguish from nitrogen deficiency: N deficiency yellows the **older lower** leaves first and is uniform across the field.

## 2.2 WHEAT (गेहूँ)

| **Yellow / stripe rust** | *Puccinia striiformis* f. sp. *tritici* | पीला रतुआ *peela ratua* | Fungal | 🔴 |

**Visual:** **Bright lemon-yellow to yellow-orange powdery pustules arranged in long, neat LINEAR STRIPES** running parallel between the leaf veins along the blade. The yellow powder **rubs off onto fingers and clothes**. Appears first as small circular "rust foci" (patches) in the field, then spreads. Cool, moist, foggy conditions (Nov–Feb) in Punjab, Haryana, HP, Uttarakhand, J&K. Can go field-wide in 10–14 days.

| **Brown / leaf rust** | *Puccinia triticina* | भूरा रतुआ *bhura ratua* | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Round-to-oval orange-brown pustules scattered RANDOMLY** (not in stripes), mainly on the **upper** leaf surface. Key differentiator from yellow rust is the **random scatter and browner colour**.

| **Black / stem rust** | *Puccinia graminis* f. sp. *tritici* | काला रतुआ *kala ratua* | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Dark reddish-brown, elongated, raised pustules on the STEM and leaf sheath**, which **rupture the epidermis leaving torn, ragged flaps of tissue** around the pustule — rough to the touch. Later turns coal-black. More a peninsular/central-India warm-area problem.

| **Loose smut** | *Ustilago segetum* var. *tritici* | कंडवा / अनावृत कंडवा *kandwa* | Fungal, seed-borne | 🔵 |

**Visual:** At ear emergence, the **whole ear head is replaced by a loose black powdery mass of spores** instead of grains; the powder blows away leaving only a **bare naked rachis (bare stick)**. Affects **scattered individual tillers**, not whole patches.

| **Karnal bunt** | *Tilletia indica* | करनाल बंट | Fungal | 🔵 (quality/export) |

**Visual:** **Partial** infection — only part of an individual grain is converted; black powder in the **groove/crease and at the embryo end** of the kernel, grain otherwise intact. Distinctive **rotten-fish / foul trimethylamine odour** when a handful of grain is rubbed.

| **Powdery mildew** | *Blumeria graminis* f. sp. *tritici* | चूर्णिल आसिता | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **White-to-grey fluffy/powdery cottony patches on the upper leaf surface and sheaths**, wipeable with a finger; later small **black dots (cleistothecia)** appear in the white mat.

| **Wheat aphids** | *Rhopalosiphum padi*, *Sitobion avenae*, *Schizaphis graminum* | चेपा / माहू *chepa / mahu* | Insect | 🟡 |

**Visual:** Dense colonies of small soft green/black insects on the **leaves and along the ear head**; sticky shiny honeydew and **black sooty mould** on the leaves below; leaves yellow and curl.

| **Termites** | *Odontotermes obesus*, *Microtermes obesi* | दीमक *deemak* | Insect | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Irregular patches of dead / drying plants that pull out with no roots** attached; roots and stem base eaten away and often **plastered with mud/soil particles**. Common in light soils and where raw FYM was applied.

## 2.3 COTTON (कपास)

| **Pink bollworm** | *Pectinophora gossypiella* | गुलाबी सुंडी *gulabi sundi*; Mr *gulabi bond ali* | Insect | 🔴 |

**Visual:** **"Rosette flower"** — the flower petals twist together and fail to open properly, giving a puckered rosette shape. On green bolls, a **tiny pinhole entry, usually plugged with excreta**, hard to spot from outside. Split the boll: a **white caterpillar with distinct pink transverse bands**, damaged locules, **stained/discoloured lint**, and **two seeds bored and stuck together**. Bolls open prematurely and partially. Currently India's #1 cotton pest following Bt resistance.

| **Whitefly** | *Bemisia tabaci* | सफेद मक्खी *safed makkhi* | Insect + virus vector | 🟠 |

**Visual:** Shake the plant — a **cloud of tiny white moth-like insects flies up** and resettles. On the **underside** of leaves, flat, scale-like, translucent-white nymphs. Leaves **sticky with honeydew**, then coated with **black sooty mould** that can be rubbed off, leaves yellow and curl downward.

| **Cotton leaf curl disease (CLCuD)** | Cotton leaf curl Multan virus / Cotton leaf curl Multan-Rajasthan virus + betasatellite; whitefly transmitted | पत्ती मरोड़ | Viral | 🟠 |

**Visual:** Leaves **curl and cup UPWARD**; **veins on the underside are thickened and darkened** (hold the leaf up to sunlight to see the swollen dark vein network); the diagnostic sign is **cup-shaped, leaf-like ENATIONS growing out of the underside of the leaf along the main veins**. Plant stunted and bushy with short internodes, few small bolls. Major in Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan.

| **Jassid / leafhopper** | *Amrasca biguttula biguttula* | हरा तेला *hara tela* | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** Leaf **margins turn yellow, then curl DOWNWARD (cupping)**, then go **bronze / brick-red / rusty** and become crisp and brittle — "hopper burn". Underside: small **wedge-shaped pale-green hoppers that walk diagonally/sideways** and jump off when disturbed. Adults have a black spot on each forewing.

| **American / gram bollworm** | *Helicoverpa armigera* | अमेरिकन सुंडी *american sundi* | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Large, clean, round bore hole in the boll with the caterpillar's head buried inside and its body hanging outside**; frass pellets around the hole. Squares and buds shed prematurely.

| **Mealybug** | *Phenacoccus solenopsis* | मिली बग / सफेद रुई कीट | Insect | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **White cottony/waxy fluffy masses in the leaf axils, on stems and growing tips** — looks like cotton wool stuck to the plant. Plant stunted, sticky, black sooty mould, leaves crumple.

## 2.4 MAIZE (मक्का)

| **Fall armyworm** | *Spodoptera frugiperda* | फॉल आर्मीवर्म / सैनिक कीट | Insect | 🔴 |

**Visual:** **Elongated papery "windowpane" scrapes on young leaves**, then **large ragged, irregular holes**; the **whorl is packed with moist, wet, sawdust-like frass pellets** — the wet frass in the whorl is the giveaway. Larva ID: **inverted white "Y" mark on the front of the head capsule**, and **four black dots arranged in a square** on the second-to-last body segment. Can shred a whorl in 2–3 days.

| **Turcicum leaf blight (Northern corn leaf blight)** | *Exserohilum turcicum* | झुलसा | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Long, boat-shaped or cigar-shaped grey-green to tan lesions, 3–15 cm long**, running **parallel to the leaf veins**, starting on the **lower older leaves** and moving up. Lesions merge and the whole leaf dries.

| **Maydis leaf blight (Southern corn leaf blight)** | *Bipolaris maydis* | — | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** Smaller than turcicum — **elongated rectangular tan lesions restricted between the veins (parallel-sided)**, often with a reddish-brown border, numerous on the leaf.

| **Banded leaf and sheath blight** | *Rhizoctonia solani* f. sp. *sasakii* | — | Fungal | 🟠 in humid areas |

**Visual:** **Alternating concentric bands of straw-coloured and dark brown discolouration on the sheaths and leaf blades**, giving a distinctly **zoned/banded** appearance; white cobweb mycelium and brown sclerotia on the sheath surface. Can reach the cob and rot it.

| **Maize stem borer** | *Chilo partellus* | तना छेदक | Insect | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Rows of pinhead "shot holes" across the unfolded leaf** (made while the leaf was still rolled in the whorl); later **dead heart** in young plants; stem tunnelled with frass extruding from holes; stems break in wind.

| **Common rust** | *Puccinia sorghi* | रतुआ | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** Small, **circular-to-elongate cinnamon-brown powdery pustules on BOTH leaf surfaces**, scattered; powder rubs off.

## 2.5 SUGARCANE (गन्ना)

| **Red rot** | *Colletotrichum falcatum* (teleomorph *Glomerella tucumanensis*) | लाल सड़न *lal sadan* | Fungal | 🔴 |

**Visual:** In the field, the **3rd or 4th leaf from the top yellows, dries and droops first** while the rest is still green. Definitive test: **split the cane lengthwise** — the internal pith is **blood-red / brick-red, crossed by distinct WHITE PATCHES arranged crosswise (perpendicular to the length)**. The cut cane gives off a **sour, alcoholic, vinegar-like smell**. Midrib lesions on leaves are red with a **straw or ash-coloured centre**. Canes become hollow, shrivelled and light. India's most destructive sugarcane disease; the CF13 pathotype has caused epidemics in UP/Bihar.

| **Smut** | *Sporisorium scitamineum* (older name *Ustilago scitaminea*) | कंडुआ / काला चाबुक *kala chabuk* ("black whip") | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** A **long, black, curved, WHIP-LIKE structure (30 cm to 1.5 m) emerging from the growing point** at the top of the cane, initially covered by a thin **silvery-white membrane** that ruptures to release masses of black powder. Affected stools are **thin, grassy, with excessive thin tillers** and narrow erect leaves.

| **Wilt** | *Fusarium sacchari* (also *Cephalosporium sacchari*) | उकठा | Fungal | 🔵 |

**Visual:** Gradual **yellowing and drying of foliage from the top downwards**; canes become **light in weight and hollow-sounding when tapped**; rind **shrinks with longitudinal wrinkles**; internally **purplish-brown to reddish-brown discolouration**, often hollow and with a mild off-smell (less sour than red rot).

| **Early shoot borer** | *Chilo infuscatellus* | अगेती तना छेदक | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** In 1–3 month old crop, **dead heart** — the central whorl of leaves dries and yellows, **pulls out very easily**, and gives off a characteristic **foul / rotten smell** (this distinguishes it from other dead-heart causes). Bore hole with frass near ground level. Worse in dry, hot conditions and light soils.

| **Top shoot borer** | *Scirpophaga excerptalis* | चोटी बेधक | Insect | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Rows of small "shot holes" in the unfolded leaves**, **red-brown streaks/tunnels along the midrib**, dead heart at the top of a grown cane, and later **"bunchy top"** — proliferation of side shoots from the top after the growing point dies.

| **Woolly aphid** | *Ceratovacuna lanigera* | सफेद ऊनी माहू | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **White cottony/woolly colonies packed on the underside of the leaves**; heavy sticky honeydew drips onto leaves below which then turn **completely black with sooty mould**; the whole field can look blackened.

| **Pokkah boeng** | *Fusarium verticillioides* / *F. moniliforme* | — | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Top leaves chlorotic, wrinkled, twisted and crumpled**, narrowed at the base, bunched at the top; in severe cases **"knife-cut" transverse slits across the stalk rind** and top rot.

## 2.6 POTATO (आलू)

| **Late blight** | *Phytophthora infestans* | पिछेती झुलसा *pichheti jhulsa* | Oomycete (fungus-like) | 🔴🔴 |

**Visual:** **Irregular, water-soaked, dark brown-to-black blotches starting at the leaf tips and margins**, surrounded by a **pale-green / yellowish halo**. On the **underside**, in a humid morning, a **fine white downy fuzzy ring of sporulation at the edge of the lesion** — the key diagnostic. Whole field can turn black and "burnt" looking and gives off a distinct **foul, rotting odour**. Stems get dark brown-black greasy lesions. Tubers: **purplish-brown sunken patches on the skin, and a granular, dry, reddish-brown rot** extending irregularly into the flesh when cut. **Can destroy a crop in 5–10 days** at 10–20 °C with fog/high humidity. Highest-urgency item in the Indian potato belt (UP, WB, Bihar, Punjab).

| **Early blight** | *Alternaria solani* | अगेती झुलसा | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Dark brown-to-black spots on the OLDER, LOWER leaves first**, each spot showing **concentric rings inside it — a "target board" / bull's-eye pattern** — with a yellow halo around it. Spots are dry and crisp, not water-soaked (opposite of late blight).

| **Black scurf / stem canker** | *Rhizoctonia solani* AG-3 | काली पपड़ी | Fungal | 🔵 |

**Visual:** **Hard, black, irregular, raised crusts stuck to the tuber skin that look like clods of dirt but DO NOT wash off** — these are sclerotia. The flesh underneath is unaffected. Underground stems and stolons show **brown, sunken, girdling cankers**; sprouts may be killed leading to gappy emergence.

| **Common scab** | *Streptomyces scabies* | खुरंड / स्केब | Bacterial (actinomycete) | 🔵 |

**Visual:** **Rough, corky, brown, raised or pitted scabby patches on the tuber surface**, often star-shaped or netted. Surface-only; flesh is fine. Worse in alkaline, dry soils.

| **Potato tuber moth** | *Phthorimaea operculella* | आलू का पतंगा | Insect | 🟡 (🔴 in storage) |

**Visual:** On leaves, **blotchy transparent mines**. On tubers, **dirty, frass-filled tunnels just under the skin**, entry usually near the eyes, with a pinkish-white caterpillar. Devastating in unrefrigerated storage.

| **Aphids (virus vectors)** | *Myzus persicae*, *Aphis gossypii* → PVY, Potato leaf roll virus | माहू | Insect / viral | 🔵 |

**Visual:** *PLRV* — lower leaves **rolled upward into a tube, stiff, leathery and papery, rattle when touched**, plant stunted and upright. *PVY* — mosaic mottling, crinkling, black streaks on stems, leaf drop.

## 2.7 TOMATO (टमाटर)

| **Late blight** | *Phytophthora infestans* | पिछेती झुलसा | Oomycete | 🔴 |

**Visual:** **Greasy, water-soaked, grey-green irregular blotches** on leaves that rapidly turn brown-black; **white downy growth on the underside** in humid mornings. On green fruit: **large, firm, greasy, brown, irregular patches with a rough/marbled surface that stay hard** (they do not soften like a rot).

| **Early blight** | *Alternaria solani* / *A. linariae* | अगेती झुलसा | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Dark brown circular spots with concentric rings ("target spot") and a yellow halo on the lower older leaves**, working upward; **collar rot** — a dark sunken girdling lesion at the stem base of seedlings; on fruit, a **dark, sunken, leathery lesion at the stem (calyx) end with concentric rings**.

| **Tomato leaf curl virus (ToLCV)** | Tomato leaf curl New Delhi virus & related begomoviruses; whitefly *Bemisia tabaci* transmitted | पत्ती मरोड़ *patti marod* | Viral | 🟠 (irreversible) |

**Visual:** Leaflets **cup and curl UPWARD**, become **small, thick, leathery and crinkled/puckered**; **yellowing along the leaf margins and between the veins**; plant severely **stunted and bushy with excessive branching and shortened internodes**; flowers drop, few or no fruits. No cure — infected plant is a permanent inoculum source.

| **Bacterial wilt** | *Ralstonia solanacearum* | जीवाणु उकठा | Bacterial | 🔴 |

**Visual:** **Sudden, complete wilting of a plant that still looks green and healthy** — no prior yellowing, which distinguishes it from fungal wilts. Cut the stem near the base: **brown discoloured vascular ring**. Field test: suspend the cut stem in a glass of clear water — **milky-white bacterial threads stream out of the cut end within minutes**. Plant dies in 2–4 days.

| **Tomato pinworm / South American leaf miner** | *Phthorimaea absoluta* (formerly *Tuta absoluta*) | टूटा | Insect | 🔴 |

**Visual:** **Irregular, blotchy, transparent mines/galleries in the leaf with visible black frass grains inside the tunnel**; leaves shrivel and go papery. On fruit, **small pinhole entries near the calyx** with frass, and internal galleries. Invaded India ~2014 and is now a top constraint.

| **Fruit borer** | *Helicoverpa armigera* | फल छेदक | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **A clean circular hole in the fruit with the caterpillar's body hanging out**, fruit hollowed and secondarily rotting; frass around the hole.

| **Septoria leaf spot** | *Septoria lycopersici* | — | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** Numerous **small circular spots with a grey-white centre, a dark brown margin, and tiny BLACK DOTS (pycnidia) visible in the centre** — the black dots are the giveaway vs early blight. ⚠ Regionally variable importance in India; more common in the hills and in humid seasons.

## 2.8 ONION (प्याज़)

| **Purple blotch** | *Alternaria porri* | बैंगनी धब्बा *baingani dhabba* | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** Starts as **small white sunken specks with a purple centre** on leaves and seed stalks; enlarge into **elongated, spindle/zonate lesions with a distinctly PURPLE-BROWN centre showing concentric rings**, bordered by a **yellow chlorotic zone that extends well above and below the lesion**. Lesions girdle the leaf and it **topples over / breaks at the lesion**. Bulbs rot at the neck (semi-watery yellow → dark red rot).

| **Stemphylium blight** | *Stemphylium vesicarium* | — | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** Small **yellow-to-orange water-soaked flecks appearing in the MIDDLE of the leaf blade** (not tip-first), elongating into **spindle-shaped brown to dark olive-brown / almost black lesions**; whole leaf blights and dies back **from the tip downward**. ⚠ Very hard to distinguish from purple blotch in a photo — Stemphylium tends to be **olive-brown/black rather than purple**, and produces a dark velvety fungal layer on dead tissue. Often the two occur together; a vision model should probably output a combined "Alternaria/Stemphylium leaf blight complex" class unless confidence is high.

| **Onion thrips** | *Thrips tabaci* | थ्रिप्स / रस चूसक कीट | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Silvery-white streaks, blotches and patches on the leaves** (where cell contents were sucked out), speckled with **tiny black faecal dots**. Leaf tips curl, dry and turn brown; leaves become twisted and distorted; plant stunted with small bulbs. The insects themselves are slender, 1–2 mm, straw-yellow to brown, **hidden deep in the leaf axils / neck** — spread the leaves apart to find them. Explodes in hot dry weather.

| **Basal rot / Fusarium** | *Fusarium oxysporum* f. sp. *cepae* | — | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Yellowing and dieback of leaves starting at the tip**, plant **lifts out of the soil with almost no roots**; the **basal plate of the bulb is rotted, brown, and covered with white fluffy mycelium**; roots pink-brown and decayed.

| **Anthracnose / "twister disease"** | *Colletotrichum gloeosporioides* | — | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Curling, twisting and abnormal elongation of the neck and leaves** (the "twister" look), chlorosis, and **water-soaked lesions bearing pink/orange (salmon) spore masses in concentric rings**. ⚠ Important in Maharashtra and Karnataka; less so in the north.

## 2.9 CHILLI (मिर्च)

| **Anthracnose / die-back / ripe fruit rot** | *Colletotrichum truncatum* (syn. *C. capsici*), *C. gloeosporioides*, *C. scovillei* | फल सड़न; Te *tamara tegulu* | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** On **fruits** — **circular, sunken, dark brown-to-black necrotic lesions with concentric rings of tiny BLACK DOT-LIKE acervuli** inside them; in humid weather **pink/salmon-coloured slimy spore masses** ooze from the dots. Infected fruits eventually **turn straw-white, papery and shrivelled**. On the plant — **die-back**: twigs blacken and die progressively **from the tip downward**, flowers and young fruits drop.

| **Chilli leaf curl / "murda" complex** | Chilli leaf curl virus (begomovirus, whitefly-borne) + *Scirtothrips dorsalis* thrips + *Polyphagotarsonemus latus* yellow/broad mite | मुरड़ा / कोकड़ा *murda / kokada* | Viral + insect + mite | 🟠 |

**Visual — worth splitting into three classes for a vision model:**
- **Thrips damage ("upward murda"):** leaf **margins curl UPWARD**, leaf becomes crinkled, boat-shaped and leathery, with **silvery-brown scarring streaks on the underside**.
- **Yellow/broad mite damage ("downward murda"):** leaf **curls DOWNWARD**, becomes **narrow, elongated, strap-like or inverted-boat-shaped**, dark green, **glossy/varnished and leathery**, with **abnormally elongated petioles**. Very distinctive.
- **Virus:** mosaic mottling, **puckered blistered leaf surface**, severe stunting, bushy shoots, small deformed fruits.

⚠ The relative contribution of virus vs thrips vs mite to "murda" is regionally contested in the Indian literature; I'd label the class as a complex.

| **Thrips** | *Scirtothrips dorsalis*; newly *Thrips parvispinus* (black flower thrips) | थ्रिप्स | Insect | 🔴 |

**Visual:** See above for leaf symptoms. ⚠ *T. parvispinus* (a newer invasive, severe in Karnataka/AP/Telangana since ~2021) additionally causes **blackening and shrivelling of flower buds, massive flower drop, blackened growing tips and stunted "burnt" looking plants** — a distinctly darker, more necrotic look than *S. dorsalis*.

| **Powdery mildew** | *Leveillula taurica* | चूर्णिल आसिता | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** Unusual for a powdery mildew — the **white powdery growth is on the LOWER leaf surface**, with corresponding **yellow blotchy patches visible on the UPPER surface**. Heavy premature leaf fall leaving bare stems with exposed fruits.

| **Damping off** | *Pythium* spp., *Rhizoctonia solani* | आर्द्र गलन | Fungal/oomycete | 🔴 (nursery only) |

**Visual:** In the nursery, **seedlings topple over in patches**; the stem at the soil line is **water-soaked, thin, constricted and brown** (pinched-in "wire stem").

## 2.10 BRINJAL (बैंगन)

| **Shoot and fruit borer** | *Leucinodes orbonalis* | तना एवं फल छेदक; Bn *begun-er poka* | Insect | 🔴 |

**Visual:** *Shoot phase* — **young growing shoot tips wilt, droop and dry out** while the rest of the plant is green and healthy; the wilted tip breaks off easily and is hollow inside. *Fruit phase* — a **round bore hole on the fruit surface plugged with dried brown excreta/frass**; on cutting the fruit, **dirty frass-filled tunnels with a pinkish-white caterpillar** inside. India's single largest brinjal constraint (commonly 40–70% fruit damage).

| **Little leaf** | *Candidatus* Phytoplasma trifolii (16SrVI); vector leafhopper *Hishimonus phycitis* | छोटी पत्ती *chhoti patti* | Phytoplasma (bacteria-like) | 🟡 (plant is a write-off) |

**Visual:** Leaves become **abnormally SMALL, narrow, thin, soft and pale yellow-green**, crowded on **drastically shortened internodes** giving a **bushy rosette / witches'-broom** appearance; excessive shoot proliferation from every axil; **flowers turn green and leafy (phyllody)** and set no fruit. Very distinctive silhouette — a short, dense, pale, bushy plant among normal ones.

| **Bacterial wilt** | *Ralstonia solanacearum* | जीवाणु उकठा | Bacterial | 🔴 |

**Visual:** **Sudden wilt of a green plant**; vascular browning at the stem base; **milky white ooze from a cut stem suspended in clear water**. Kills in days; very serious in eastern/southern India.

| **Phomopsis blight & fruit rot** | *Phomopsis vexans* | — | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** Grey-brown **circular leaf spots with an ash centre and scattered black pinhead pycnidia**; on fruits, **pale, soft, sunken, watery blotches** that enlarge, then the fruit blackens, shrivels and **mummifies while still hanging**.

| **Jassid / whitefly / mites** | *Amrasca biguttula biguttula*, *Bemisia tabaci*, *Tetranychus* spp. | तेला, सफेद मक्खी, माइट | Insect / mite | 🟡 |

**Visual:** Jassid — downward leaf-margin curling turning bronze-red. Mites — fine **stippling/speckling, dull bronze underside, and fine webbing** in leaf axils.

## 2.11 OKRA / BHINDI (भिंडी)

| **Yellow vein mosaic disease** | Bhendi yellow vein mosaic virus (begomovirus); *Bemisia tabaci* | पीला शिरा मोज़ेक *peela shira mosaic* | Viral | 🟠 (irreversible) |

**Visual:** **The vein network turns bright golden-yellow while the tissue between the veins stays green** — producing an unmistakable **yellow reticulate net / lace pattern** on the leaf. Progresses until the whole leaf blade is yellow-white. **Fruits are small, pale yellowish-white, hard and deformed**, entirely unmarketable. The single most recognisable viral symptom in Indian vegetables.

| **Enation leaf curl disease** | Okra enation leaf curl virus + betasatellite; whitefly-borne | — | Viral | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Tiny pinhead-sized warty outgrowths (ENATIONS) on the UNDERSIDE of the leaf**, making the leaf **rough and sandpapery to the touch**; leaf becomes **thick, leathery and curled**, veins thicken, **petioles bend and twist at odd angles**; plant severely stunted; fruits small, twisted and deformed. ⚠ In north India both YVMV and enation symptoms commonly appear on the same plant; in south India they tend to separate.

| **Shoot and fruit borer** | *Earias vittella*, *E. insulana* | तना एवं फल छेदक | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** Before fruiting — **top shoots wilt and droop** with a bore hole and frass. After fruiting — **bore holes on the pods filled with frass**; fruits become **twisted, bent and deformed**, and are hollow inside with a **brownish/greenish caterpillar with a spiny body**.

| **Jassid / leafhopper** | *Amrasca biguttula biguttula* | हरा तेला | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** Leaf **margins yellow → curl DOWNWARD (cup shape) → turn bronze/reddish-brown and crisp** ("hopper burn"); plants stunted. Pale-green wedge-shaped hoppers on the underside.

| **Powdery mildew** | *Erysiphe cichoracearum* / *Podosphaera xanthii* | चूर्णिल आसिता | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **White powdery talc-like patches on the UPPER leaf surface**, coalescing to coat the whole leaf; leaves then yellow, dry and fall.

## 2.12 SOYBEAN (सोयाबीन)

| **Yellow mosaic disease** | Mungbean yellow mosaic India virus (MYMIV); *Bemisia tabaci* | पीला मोज़ेक | Viral | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Bright yellow irregular patches and flecks intermixed with normal green** on the leaf, giving a **mosaic / marbled** look; patches enlarge until the whole leaf is yellow, then turn **necrotic brown at the margins**. Pods **few, small, curved and deformed** with shrivelled seed. Major in MP, Maharashtra, Rajasthan.

| **Girdle beetle** | *Obereopsis brevis* | गर्डल बीटल / चक्र भृंग | Insect | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Two parallel ring-like cuts girdling the stem or petiole a few centimetres apart** — the part **above the girdle wilts and dries into a brown flag while the rest of the plant stays green**. Highly distinctive and easy for a model to learn. Split the stem below the girdle to find the grub tunnelling downward.

| **Stem fly** | *Melanagromyza sojae* | तना मक्खी | Insect | 🟡 |

**Visual:** Seedlings wilt and die; older plants show **reddish-brown tunnels in the pith when the stem is split lengthwise**, with a small white maggot.

| **Defoliators — tobacco caterpillar & semilooper** | *Spodoptera litura*; *Chrysodeixis acuta*, *Thysanoplusia orichalcea* | तंबाकू की सुंडी *tambaku ki sundi* | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Leaves skeletonised — only the vein network remains**, whole plants stripped in patches. *Spodoptera* egg masses are **brown, fluffy, hair-covered pads on the leaf underside**; young larvae feed gregariously making the leaf papery-white. Semiloopers are green and **arch their body into a loop when walking**.

| **Charcoal rot / dry root rot** | *Macrophomina phaseolina* | — | Fungal | 🟡 (🟠 in drought) |

**Visual:** Plant dries prematurely; the **stem base and taproot turn light grey/silvery**; scrape the bark and the wood underneath is **peppered with countless tiny black pinpoint microsclerotia, like ground charcoal dust**.

| **Soybean rust** | *Phakopsora pachyrhizi* | रतुआ | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Small angular tan/brown flecks on the upper leaf surface**, with **tiny raised tan-to-reddish-brown volcano-like pustules on the LOWER surface**; severe premature defoliation from the bottom up. ⚠ Historically most severe in south India and the hills; incidence in the central Indian plains varies year to year — verify locally.

## 2.13 GROUNDNUT (मूंगफली)

| **Early leaf spot (Tikka)** | *Passalora arachidicola* (syn. *Cercospora arachidicola*) | टिक्का रोग *tikka rog* | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Circular reddish-brown to dark brown spots, 1–10 mm across, on the UPPER leaflet surface, each ringed by a bright YELLOW HALO**; the corresponding spot on the underside is a lighter brown. Also on petioles/stems as elongated elliptical spots. Appears from ~35 days after sowing.

| **Late leaf spot (Tikka)** | *Nothopassalora personata* (syn. *Cercosporidium personatum*, *Phaeoisariopsis personata*) | टिक्का रोग | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Nearly BLACK, rough, circular spots, denser and darker on the LOWER leaflet surface**, usually with **little or NO yellow halo** (or only a faint one). The rough black surface bearing spore tufts is the key differentiator from early leaf spot. Causes **heavy defoliation — leaflets drop leaving bare petioles**.

| **Rust** | *Puccinia arachidis* | रतुआ | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Orange-brown / cinnamon-coloured powdery pustules mainly on the LOWER leaf surface**, which rupture and shed rusty powder onto the fingers. Diagnostic difference from leaf spot: **rust-affected leaves dry up but REMAIN ATTACHED to the plant**, whereas leaf spot causes them to fall off.

| **Stem rot / collar rot** | *Sclerotium rolfsii* | — | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **White, silky, fan-shaped mycelial mat spreading over the stem base and adjacent soil**, studded with **round mustard-seed-sized sclerotia that mature from white → tan → dark brown**. Plant wilts, and **pods detach in the soil and are left behind at harvest**.

| **Aspergillus crown rot** | *Aspergillus niger* | — | Fungal | 🔴 (seedling) |

**Visual:** Seedlings collapse; the **collar/hypocotyl region is rotted and covered in a black sooty powdery spore mass**.

| **Bud necrosis disease (PBND)** | Groundnut bud necrosis orthotospovirus; thrips-transmitted (*Thrips palmi*, *Frankliniella schultzei*) | कली अंगमारी | Viral | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Chlorotic rings, spots and mottling on young leaflets**, followed by **blackening and death of the terminal bud (necrosis)**; the plant then throws out a mass of **stunted axillary shoots with small, distorted leaflets**; severe stunting, few pods.

| **Groundnut leaf miner** | *Aproaerema modicella* | पत्ती सुरंगक | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Small transparent blotch mines on the leaflets**, then the **leaflets are webbed together and rolled into a bunch**, turning brown and papery. Heavily infested fields look **scorched/burnt from a distance**.

## 2.14 MUSTARD / RAPESEED (सरसों)

| **Mustard aphid** | *Lipaphis erysimi* | चेपा / माहू *chepa / mahu*; Pa *tela* | Insect | 🔴 |

**Visual:** **Dense grey-green colonies of soft-bodied aphids completely encrusting the growing tips, the flowering raceme and the developing pods** — the inflorescence looks thickened, grey and fuzzy. Shoots curl and stunt, honeydew makes everything sticky, and **black sooty mould** develops. Pods fail to fill. India's single most damaging mustard pest — can wreck a crop in 1–2 weeks at flowering.

| **Alternaria blight** | *Alternaria brassicae*, *A. brassicicola* | झुलसा रोग | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Greyish-black to dark brown circular spots with CONCENTRIC RINGS (target pattern) on the lower leaves first**, each with a yellow halo; spots coalesce into large blighted patches and the leaf drops. On pods, **dark sunken elongated spots** which make the pods **shrivelled and constricted**, seeds small and shrunken.

| **White rust** | *Albugo candida* | सफेद रतुआ / सफेद गेरुई | Oomycete | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Shiny, raised, WHITE-to-creamy blister-like pustules on the LOWER leaf surface**, which burst and release a chalky white powder; a corresponding yellowish patch shows on the upper surface. Systemic infection produces the classic **"STAG HEAD"** — the inflorescence axis becomes **swollen, thickened, curved and grotesquely distorted like a deer's antler**, green and fleshy, bearing sterile malformed flowers and no seed. Highly diagnostic.

| **Sclerotinia stem rot** | *Sclerotinia sclerotiorum* | तना गलन | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Water-soaked then BLEACHED WHITE-GREY lesions on the stem**, often at a branch fork; the stem **shreds into fibres** and the plant above suddenly dries and dies while the rest of the field is green. Split the stem: **hard, irregular, black sclerotia like rat droppings inside the hollow pith**, sometimes with fluffy white mycelium. Increasingly serious in Rajasthan, Haryana, Punjab.

| **Downy mildew** | *Hyaloperonospora brassicae* | मृदुरोमिल आसिता | Oomycete | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Greyish-white downy fuzzy growth on the LOWER leaf surface** with **yellowish angular patches bounded by veins** on the upper surface. Frequently co-occurs with white rust on the same stag-headed inflorescence.

| **Painted bug** | *Bagrada hilaris* | चितकबरा बग | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** Shield-shaped bugs, **~5 mm, boldly patterned black with orange/red and white markings**, clustered on seedlings (Oct–Nov) and again on drying pods at harvest. Seedlings wilt and die; seeds shrivelled.

## 2.15 CHICKPEA / GRAM (चना)

| **Fusarium wilt** | *Fusarium oxysporum* f. sp. *ciceris* | उकठा *ukatha* / मुरझान | Fungal | 🟡 (no rescue) |

**Visual:** **Drooping of petioles, rachis and leaflets**, then the plant turns dull green then **straw-yellow and dries out completely, often only on ONE SIDE / one branch first**. The **roots show NO external rot** (unlike root rots) — but **split the taproot/collar lengthwise and a continuous dark brown-to-black discoloured vascular strand runs through it**. Occurs in expanding patches in the field.

| **Ascochyta blight** | *Ascochyta rabiei* | — | Fungal | 🔴 |

**Visual:** **Round-to-elongated brown lesions on leaves, stems and pods with dark margins and CONCENTRIC RINGS OF TINY BLACK PYCNIDIA (dots) inside**, often arranged in circles. Stem lesions **girdle the stem so the plant breaks and hangs over**. Spreads as **rapidly expanding circular dead patches in the field after rain**. Mainly a north-west Indian (Punjab, Haryana, HP, hills) cool-wet-weather disease.

| **Dry root rot** | *Rhizoctonia bataticola* / *Macrophomina phaseolina* | जड़ सड़न | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Sudden drying of scattered plants with straw-coloured leaves that stay attached**; pull the plant — the **taproot is brittle and snaps off short**, with **most lateral roots gone**. The **bark peels off easily** and the wood underneath is dark and **studded with minute black pinpoint sclerotia**. Triggered by warm dry conditions / terminal drought.

| **Gram pod borer** | *Helicoverpa armigera* | चना फली छेदक *chana phali chhedak* | Insect | 🔴 |

**Visual:** A **green-to-brown caterpillar with pale longitudinal stripes, its head thrust inside a pod and its body outside**; a **clean circular hole in the pod wall**; seed eaten out. Also feeds on buds, flowers and tender leaves early on. India's most economically damaging pulse pest.

| **Botrytis grey mould** | *Botrytis cinerea* | धूसर फफूंदी | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Grey-brown, fuzzy, felty mould on flowers, buds, and stem nodes**, in dense humid canopies; flowers rot and drop, **no pod set**, and shoot tips die back. Serious in the north-east plains (Bihar, WB, eastern UP) and Nepal terai.

| **Collar rot** | *Sclerotium rolfsii* | — | Fungal | 🟠 (seedling) |

**Visual:** Seedling collar covered with **white silky mycelium and mustard-seed-like sclerotia**; plant topples.

## 2.16 PIGEON PEA / ARHAR-TUR (अरहर/तूर)

| **Fusarium wilt** | *Fusarium udum* | उकठा *ukatha* | Fungal | 🟡 |

**Visual:** Progressive yellowing and wilting, classically affecting **ONE SIDE of the plant only (partial wilt)** — half the canopy dries while the other half stays green. **Diagnostic sign: a dark PURPLE-BROWN to BLACK BAND running upward from the base of the stem on the affected side**, visible on the outside of the stem. Internal xylem blackened. Usually strikes at flowering/podding.

| **Sterility mosaic disease** | Pigeonpea sterility mosaic emaravirus; vector eriophyid mite *Aceria cajani* | बांझपन रोग *banjhpan rog*; "green plague" | Viral (mite-transmitted) | 🟡 |

**Visual:** A **bushy, pale-green, vigorously vegetative plant that produces NO flowers and NO pods at all** while neighbouring plants are podding — this contrast is the diagnosis. Leaves are **reduced in size, crowded, and show light-and-dark green mosaic mottling**; excessive branching gives a dense bushy shape. Nicknamed the "green plague" because the plant looks healthy but yields zero.

| **Phytophthora blight** | *Phytophthora drechsleri* f. sp. *cajani* | — | Oomycete | 🔴 |

**Visual:** **Dark brown-to-black, water-soaked, sunken lesions girdling the stem**, most often at the collar or at a node; the plant **snaps at the lesion and dries suddenly**, often within days of heavy rain or waterlogging. Occurs in wet low-lying patches.

| **Pod borer complex** | *Helicoverpa armigera*; *Maruca vitrata* (spotted pod borer); *Melanagromyza obtusa* (pod fly) | फली छेदक *phali chhedak* | Insect | 🔴 |

**Visual — three distinct looks:**
- *Helicoverpa*: clean **round hole in the pod, larva half in/half out**, frass outside.
- *Maruca*: **flowers, buds and pods webbed together into a silk-and-frass bundle**, with the larva (whitish with paired black spots on each segment) hiding inside the web; the webbing is the giveaway.
- *Pod fly*: **NO external hole visible** until a tiny pinhole exit appears; open an apparently healthy pod and the **seeds are shrivelled, discoloured and partially eaten** with a small white maggot or pupa inside. Causes large hidden losses.

## 2.17 BANANA (केला)

| **Panama wilt / Fusarium wilt** (incl. **TR4**) | *Fusarium oxysporum* f. sp. *cubense*, VCG 0124/0125/01220 etc.; TR4 on Cavendish/Grand Naine | पनामा उकठा | Fungal | 🔵 per plant, 🔴 strategically |

**Visual:** **Progressive YELLOWING of the OLDER, LOWER leaves starting at the leaf margins** and moving inward; the yellowed leaves **collapse and hang down around the pseudostem like a dry skirt while remaining attached at the petiole**. **Longitudinal splitting/cracking of the outer sheaths of the pseudostem at the base**. Definitive: **cut the pseudostem or corm across — reddish-brown to dark yellow-brown discoloured vascular strands appear as dots/rings in the cut face**. TR4 was first confirmed in India in Bihar (Katihar) in 2015 and has spread to UP, WB, Gujarat, Maharashtra, MP. Soil stays infested for decades — highest strategic urgency of any Indian banana problem.

| **Sigatoka leaf spot** | Yellow: *Mycosphaerella musicola*; Black leaf streak: *Pseudocercospora fijiensis* | सिगाटोका | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** Begins as **small pale-yellow (yellow Sigatoka) or reddish-brown to near-black (black Sigatoka) narrow STREAKS running PARALLEL to the leaf veins**; these enlarge into **elliptical/oval spots with a light grey or ash-coloured centre, a dark brown-black border, and a yellow halo**. Spots coalesce so the **leaf dries in from the margins and tip**, leaving only a few functional leaves and small bunches.

| **Banana bunchy top virus (BBTV)** | Babuvirus; vector banana aphid *Pentalonia nigronervosa* | गुच्छ शीर्ष | Viral | 🟡 (plant is a write-off) |

**Visual:** The earliest and best sign is **dark green, irregular DOT-AND-DASH streaks along the leaf midrib, secondary veins and petiole — "MORSE CODE" streaking** — best seen by holding the leaf up to the light. Successive new leaves emerge **progressively shorter, narrower and more upright**, with **wavy, chlorotic, brittle margins**, until the top of the plant is a tight **rosette / "bunchy top"**. Plant severely stunted, no bunch or a tiny distorted one. Suckers from an infected mat are all diseased.

| **Pseudostem weevil / Rhizome weevil** | *Odoiporus longicollis* / *Cosmopolites sordidus* | तना घुन / प्रकंद घुन | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Pinhead-sized holes on the pseudostem or corm, oozing a gummy, jelly-like, brownish exudate**. Split the pseudostem: **blackened, foul, frass-filled tunnels** with fat, legless, creamy grubs with brown heads. Plant weakens and **snaps or topples in wind**.

| **Banana bract mosaic / Infectious chlorosis** | Banana bract mosaic virus; Cucumber mosaic virus | कोकन रोग | Viral | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Spindle-shaped dark green, reddish or brownish streaks on the bracts of the flower bunch and on the pseudostem**, and chlorotic streaks on the petioles. ⚠ Predominantly a south Indian problem (TN, Kerala, AP).

## 2.18 MANGO (आम)

| **Powdery mildew** | *Erysiphe quercicola* / *Oidium mangiferae* | चूर्णिल आसिता / भभूतिया रोग | Fungal | 🔴 (at flowering) |

**Visual:** **White-to-greyish powdery talc-like coating on the panicle stalks, the flowers, tender young leaves and pea-sized fruitlets** — looks as if ash has been dusted on. Affected flowers and fruitlets **blacken and drop en masse**, leaving a **bare, blackened panicle skeleton**. Can wipe out an entire flowering in 1–2 weeks in cool cloudy weather; the single most urgent flowering-stage mango problem in north and west India.

| **Anthracnose** | *Colletotrichum gloeosporioides*, *C. asianum* etc. | श्यामवर्ण / एन्थ्रेक्नोज | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** On **leaves** — irregular **dark brown-to-black spots that merge into large necrotic blotches**, often with cracking and shot-holes. On **panicles** — **blackening of the flower stalk and dieback**, heavy flower drop. On **fruit** — **sunken black lesions, frequently in a distinctive "TEAR-STAIN" pattern of dark streaks running down from the stem end**; in humid weather, **pink/orange spore masses** appear in the lesion centre. Infection is latent and erupts as **black soft rot as the fruit ripens** post-harvest.

| **Mango hopper** | *Idioscopus nitidulus*, *I. clypealis*, *Amritodus atkinsoni* | भुनगा / फुदका *bhunga / phudka* | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Wedge-shaped brownish hoppers swarming densely on the flowering panicles**, jumping and flying off with a **characteristic hissing/rustling sound** when the branch is shaken. Panicles dry and drop. Copious **sticky honeydew rains down onto the leaves below**, which then become **coated with black sooty mould — a black film that can be scraped off with a fingernail**, leaving green leaf underneath.

| **Mango malformation** | *Fusarium mangiferae* (mite involvement debated ⚠) | आम का गुच्छा रोग / मैलफॉर्मेशन | Fungal | 🔵 |

**Visual:** *Vegetative* — a **compact bunched rosette of short, thickened, swollen shoots bearing tiny scale-like leaves**, like a witch's broom at the branch tip. *Floral* — the panicle is a **dense, compact, heavily branched greenish mass with thickened axes bearing mostly male flowers**; it sets little or no fruit and **stays on the tree drying and blackened**. Predominantly north India (Punjab, Delhi, western UP, Bihar). ⚠ Aetiology (fungus vs mite vs hormonal) is still debated in the literature.

| **Mango fruit fly** | *Bactrocera dorsalis*, *B. zonata* | फल मक्खी *phal makkhi* | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** A **small oviposition puncture on the ripening fruit surrounded by a dark, sunken, necrotic ring**, sometimes with **clear sap oozing and crystallising**. Cut the fruit: the **pulp is a brown, sour, fermenting mush containing white legless maggots that flip/jump** when disturbed. Major quarantine barrier for exports.

| **Mango mealybug** | *Drosicha mangiferae* | गुजिया कीट | Insect | 🟡 |

**Visual:** In winter/spring, **white waxy oval insects crawling in columns up the trunk** and massing on the panicles; heavy honeydew and sooty mould.

## 2.19 GRAPE (अंगूर)

| **Downy mildew** | *Plasmopara viticola* | डाउनी मिल्ड्यू / केवड़ा रोग | Oomycete | 🔴🔴 |

**Visual:** On the **upper leaf surface, pale-yellow translucent "OIL SPOTS"** — irregular patches that look greasy/oily when the leaf is held up to light. Directly **beneath each oil spot, on the underside, a dense WHITE COTTONY DOWNY growth** appears overnight in humid conditions — the diagnostic pairing. Older spots turn brown and give a **mosaic/tiled** look. On **bunches**: berries turn **dull grey-brown, leathery and shrivel** ("brown rot"/"grey rot"); young bunches get covered in white down and drop. Shoot tips curl, thicken and get coated white. **The top grape emergency in Nashik/Sangli** — one rain event at 18–25 °C and >85 % RH can destroy a bunch crop in days.

| **Powdery mildew** | *Erysiphe necator* (syn. *Uncinula necator*) | पाउडरी मिल्ड्यू / भभूतिया | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Greyish-white dusty/talcum-powder coating on the UPPER leaf surface, shoots, rachis and berries**, with a musty odour. Affected **berries stop enlarging, turn dull, and CRACK / SPLIT OPEN exposing the seeds** — the cracking is the key fruit sign. Note: powdery mildew favours **warm, dry** conditions — the opposite of downy mildew, which is a useful disambiguator.

| **Anthracnose / bird's-eye rot** | *Elsinoe ampelina* | बर्ड्स आई रॉट | Fungal | 🟠 |

**Visual:** On **berries**, small **circular sunken spots with an ASH-GREY/WHITE CENTRE and a raised dark purple-brown to black margin — resembling a BIRD'S EYE**. On leaves, small brown spots whose **centres fall out leaving clean shot-holes**. On shoots, elongated **sunken dark cankers** that can girdle the shoot. Monsoon disease.

| **Bacterial leaf spot / bacterial canker** | *Xanthomonas campestris* pv. *viticola* | जीवाणु धब्बा | Bacterial | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Small, water-soaked, ANGULAR black spots on the leaf bounded by the veins, each with a yellow halo**; **black raised streaks and cankers running along the petioles, shoots and the bunch rachis**; the rachis blackens and the bunch dries. ⚠ Can be confused with anthracnose — bacterial spots are **angular and vein-limited with yellow halos**, anthracnose spots are **round with grey centres/shot-holes**. Serious in Maharashtra and north Karnataka.

| **Thrips** | *Scirtothrips dorsalis*, *Rhipiphorothrips cruentatus* | थ्रिप्स | Insect | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **Silvery-brown scarring and speckling on leaves**; on berries, a distinctive **corky brown "RING / HALO" scar around the stalk end (pedicel)** — a market-rejection blemish rather than a yield loss.

| **Mealybug** | *Maconellicoccus hirsutus* | मिली बग | Insect | 🟡 |

**Visual:** **White cottony waxy masses packed inside the bunch and in leaf axils**, honeydew and black sooty mould making the bunch unmarketable.

## 2.20 CITRUS (संतरा / मौसंबी / नींबू)

| **Huanglongbing / citrus greening** | *Candidatus* Liberibacter asiaticus; vector Asian citrus psyllid *Diaphorina citri* | ग्रीनिंग / हरितिमा रोग | Bacterial (phloem-limited) | 🔵 per tree, 🔴 for the orchard |

**Visual:** The key leaf sign is **BLOTCHY MOTTLE — irregular, diffuse yellow patches on the leaf blade that are ASYMMETRIC, i.e. the pattern on one side of the midrib does NOT mirror the other side**. This asymmetry is what separates HLB from **zinc/iron deficiency, whose interveinal yellowing is symmetric and sharply vein-delimited** — the single most important distinction for a vision model to learn. Other signs: **individual yellow shoots standing out in an otherwise green canopy**; **corky, enlarged, protruding leaf veins**; fruits **small, LOPSIDED/asymmetric, remaining green at the stylar (bottom) end when the rest colours**, with **aborted dark brown seeds** and a bitter, salty taste; premature fruit drop, twig dieback, general decline. No cure — infected trees must be removed. Widespread in Nagpur mandarin, Punjab kinnow, AP sweet orange.

| **Citrus canker** | *Xanthomonas citri* subsp. *citri* | कैंकर / नासूर | Bacterial | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Raised, corky, tan-to-brown ERUPTIVE lesions on leaves, twigs and fruits**, with a **sunken crater-like centre**, a **water-soaked greasy margin**, and a surrounding **bright YELLOW HALO**. Lesions are **rough and can be felt with a fingernail** — texture is diagnostic. Blemishes the fruit rind without rotting it; causes defoliation and premature fruit drop. Spread by wind-driven rain; **much worse where leaf miner has made wounds**. Acid lime (kagzi nimbu) is the most susceptible.

| **Gummosis / Phytophthora foot rot** | *Phytophthora nicotianae*, *P. palmivora*, *P. citrophthora* | गोंदिया रोग *gondiya rog* | Oomycete | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Amber/honey-coloured gum oozing from the trunk near ground level and hardening into brittle brown lumps**; the **bark cracks vertically, turns dark, and peels away** revealing **brown, dead, water-soaked tissue underneath**. Canopy thins, leaves show **yellowing along the midrib and veins**, twigs die back, fruit small.

| **Citrus leaf miner** | *Phyllocnistis citrella* | पत्ती सुरंगक | Insect | 🟠 |

**Visual:** **Silvery, shiny, SERPENTINE (snaking) tunnels just beneath the surface of young flush leaves**, each mine with a **thin dark central frass line running down the middle** — very distinctive. Leaves become **curled, crumpled, distorted and hardened**. Important less for direct damage than because **the wounds are the entry point for citrus canker**.

| **Citrus psylla** | *Diaphorina citri* | सिट्रस सिल्ला | Insect | 🔴 (as HLB vector) |

**Visual:** Small (3 mm) **mottled brown adults that sit on tender shoots at a distinctive head-down 30–45° ANGLE, tail raised**, and jump when disturbed. **Yellow-orange flat nymphs on the flush secreting white waxy curly tubules/threads**. Shoot tips distorted, twisted and dried. The primary reason to spray in an HLB-endemic block.

| **Citrus tristeza virus (CTV)** | Closterovirus; aphid vectors *Toxoptera citricida*, *Aphis gossypii* | ट्रिस्टेजा | Viral | 🟡–🔵 |

**Visual:** **Vein clearing / vein flecking in young leaves** — hold the leaf up to the light and the veins appear translucent; **cupped, small, chlorotic leaves**; **stem pitting** — peel the bark and the wood underneath shows **elongated pits/grooves**; a **honeycombed and swollen bud-union**. Quick decline of trees on sour orange rootstock.

# PART 3 — Cross-Crop Look-Alikes & Confusion Traps

Worth encoding explicitly for a vision model, because these are the most common misdiagnoses:

| Confusion | How to tell apart |
|---|---|
| **HLB blotchy mottle vs Zn/Fe deficiency (citrus)** | HLB mottle is **asymmetric across the midrib**; deficiency is **symmetric and vein-delimited**. |
| **Rice blast vs brown spot** | Blast = **spindle-shaped, pointed ends, grey centre**. Brown spot = **round/oval, evenly scattered, sesame-seed sized**. |
| **Rice BLB vs leaf tip drying from salinity/K deficiency** | BLB has a **wavy irregular margin** and morning **bacterial ooze**; abiotic tip-burn has a **straight, even boundary**. |
| **Late blight vs early blight (potato/tomato)** | Late = **water-soaked, greasy, tip/margin-first, white down underneath**. Early = **dry, concentric target rings, lower older leaves first**. |
| **Yellow rust vs brown rust (wheat)** | Yellow = **linear stripes, lemon-yellow**. Brown = **random scatter, orange-brown**. |
| **Onion purple blotch vs Stemphylium blight** | Purple = **purple centre, tip-first**. Stemphylium = **olive/black-brown, starts mid-leaf**. Often co-occur — consider a merged class. |
| **Chilli thrips vs mite murda** | Thrips = leaves curl **UPWARD**. Broad mite = leaves curl **DOWNWARD**, narrow, glossy, elongated petioles. |
| **Grape downy vs powdery mildew** | Downy = **oil spot + white down UNDERNEATH**, in wet weather. Powdery = **grey dust on TOP**, in dry weather; berries **crack**. |
| **Sooty mould vs a fungal disease** | Sooty mould is **superficial black film that wipes/scrapes off**, leaving healthy green tissue — it is a *symptom of a sucking pest* (whitefly/aphid/mealybug/hopper), not a pathogen. |
| **Viral leaf curl vs herbicide (2,4-D) drift** | Herbicide damage = **strap-like, parallel-veined, fan-shaped new growth, uniform along a field edge/wind direction**; virus = **patchy, random plants, with mottling and vein changes**. |
| **Wilt (Fusarium) vs bacterial wilt** | Fusarium = **gradual yellowing then drying, vascular browning, often one-sided**. Bacterial = **sudden collapse of a still-green plant + milky ooze in water test**. |
| **Nutrient deficiencies** | **N** — uniform pale yellowing of **older/lower** leaves first. **K** — **scorching/necrosis of leaf margins and tips** of older leaves. **Zn** — **interveinal chlorosis + small narrow rosetted leaves, shortened internodes**, on **young** leaves. **Fe** — **sharp interveinal chlorosis with veins staying bright green**, on the **youngest** leaves. **S** — uniform yellowing of the **youngest** leaves. **Mg** — interveinal chlorosis of **older** leaves, often with a reddish-purple tinge. |

# Uncertainty Flags — Please Verify Before Production Use

1. **Crop area figures** are approximate. I could not retrieve the GoI area tables (Economic Survey Tab 1.16 returned 403; ASG 2024 PDF too large). Rankings are solid; decimals are not.
2. **Regional crop names** have spelling and transliteration variants and district-level synonyms. The Hindi Devanagari and the Tamil/Telugu/Kannada/Marathi names given are the widely accepted ones, but I did not verify every single one against a linguistic authority. Bengali and Punjabi entries are the least verified.
3. **Pest/disease Hindi names**: many insect pests have no standardised Hindi name and farmers use English or purely descriptive terms. I have given names only where they are genuinely in common use (e.g. *gulabi sundi*, *chepa*, *ukatha*, *lal sadan*, *deemak*). Do not treat blanks as gaps to be filled by invention.
4. **Scientific names have been revised** for several taxa — I have given the current accepted name with the older synonym in brackets where farmers/extension literature still uses the old one (*Cercospora arachidicola* → *Passalora arachidicola*; *Uncinula necator* → *Erysiphe necator*; *Ustilago scitaminea* → *Sporisorium scitamineum*; *Tuta absoluta* → *Phthorimaea absoluta*; *Pyricularia*/*Magnaporthe oryzae*).
5. **Contested aetiologies**: chilli "murda" complex (virus vs thrips vs mite contribution), mango malformation (*Fusarium mangiferae* vs mite vs physiological).
6. **Regional distribution caveats** noted inline: soybean rust prevalence in central India; banana bract mosaic (south India); Ascochyta blight (NW India only); Botrytis grey mould of chickpea (NE plains); onion twister disease (Maharashtra/Karnataka).
7. **Onion purple blotch vs Stemphylium blight** are genuinely difficult to separate photographically and commonly co-occur — I recommend a merged class in a vision model unless training data is very clean.
8. **TNAU detail pages**: only the rice blast page could be fetched in full; the crop-protection index pages contain no symptom text. If you want verbatim TNAU symptom wording for other crops, the individual `agritech.tnau.ac.in/expert_system/<crop>/cpdis<name>.html` pages need to be enumerated and fetched one by one. Note `www.agritech.tnau.ac.in` fails TLS — use the bare `agritech.tnau.ac.in` host.
