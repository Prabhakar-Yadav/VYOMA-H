**What the farmer gets**
Subsidy to buy farm machinery and implements — tractors, power tillers, rotavators, seed drills, planters, threshers, harvesters, plant protection equipment and self-propelled machines.
Assistance is 40 to 50 percent of the machine cost, depending on the category of farmer.
Small and marginal farmers, women farmers, and farmers of Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe communities get the higher rate.

For groups:
- Custom Hiring Centres and hi-tech hubs — 40 percent of project cost for rural entrepreneurs, cooperative societies, registered farmer societies, FPOs and panchayats
- Village level Farm Machinery Banks — 80 percent of project cost for projects up to Rupees 30 lakh, for cooperative societies, registered farmer societies, FPOs, Self Help Groups and panchayats

Machinery can also simply be hired through a Custom Hiring Centre, without buying anything. The FARMS app lists nearby centres and available machines.

**Who qualifies**
Individual farmers who own or cultivate land qualify for machinery subsidy.
Registered groups qualify for Custom Hiring Centres and Farm Machinery Banks.

**How to apply**
1. Register on agrimachinery.nic.in, or on the state's own farm mechanisation portal, or through the district agriculture office or the Common Service Centre.
2. Select the machine or implement from the approved list.
3. Submit the application within the state's application window. Many states allot by lottery when applications exceed the target.
4. Wait for the permit or work order.
5. Buy the machine from an empanelled dealer and keep the invoice.
6. A department officer physically verifies the machine and records the engine and chassis number.
7. The subsidy is credited to the bank account by direct benefit transfer.

**Documents needed**
- Aadhaar
- Land record
- Bank passbook, Aadhaar-linked
- Caste certificate where the higher subsidy rate applies
- Passport photograph
- Dealer quotation, and the invoice after purchase

**Deadline pattern**
⚠ States open and close application windows on their own calendar, often at the start of the financial year and again before the main sowing season. Targets are limited and fill quickly.
