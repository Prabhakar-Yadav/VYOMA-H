### Why it matters
One insect species attacking cotton, chickpea, pigeonpea, tomato, chilli, sorghum, maize, sunflower,
groundnut and many vegetables — so the same advice reaches farmers across almost every cropping
system. TNAU treats "chilli fruit borer" and "gram pod borer" as the same species, referring the
reader back to the cotton entry.
(eagri ENTO 331 Lecture 22 — http://eagri.org/eagri50/ENTO331/lecture22/chillies/004.html)

### Host range
Cotton, sorghum, lablab, pea, chillies, groundnut, tobacco, okra, maize, tomato, soybean,
safflower, chickpea (gram), pigeonpea (redgram), sunflower, and many more — over 180 plant species.
(eagri ENTO 331 Lecture 06 — http://eagri.org/eagri50/ENTO331/lecture06/001.html)

### Life cycle and stage durations
- **Egg**: laid **singly on the host plant**, on the youngest tissue — growing points, flower buds,
  tender leaves and the calyx of developing fruit. Spherical, ribbed, creamy white turning brown.
  A female lays roughly **500–1000 eggs** over her life. **Egg period 7 days.** ⚠ Under warm Indian
  field conditions the egg period is often reported as 2–4 days; the TNAU teaching figure of 7 days
  is a laboratory average.
- **Larva**: full-grown larva about 2 inches (**40–50 mm**) long, greenish with dark brown-grey
  lines and dark and pale longitudinal bands; **colour is highly variable from greenish to brown to
  almost black**, so colour is unreliable for identification. The reliable characters are the
  **light and dark longitudinal stripes** and the **rough skin with microspines** (visible as a
  gritty texture under a lens). **Larval duration 14 days**, in 5–6 instars.
- **Pupa**: **pupates in the SOIL**, in an earthen cell, at a depth of about **2.5–17 cm**.
  **Pupal period 10 days.**
- **Adult**: moth greenish to brown with a **'V'-shaped speck on the forewings** and a **dull black
  border on the hind wing**. Nocturnal, strong flier.
- **One generation is completed in 28 days under favourable conditions.**
  **About 8 generations per year** in India, overlapping. ⚠
(eagri ENTO 331 Lecture 06 — http://eagri.org/eagri50/ENTO331/lecture06/001.html)

### Where each stage sits — and what this means for action
- Eggs and small larvae: **on the surface of the youngest growth** — this is the exposed window and
  the only stage reachable by egg parasitoids and by anything applied to the plant.
- Larger larvae: **head thrust inside the pod/boll/fruit with the rest of the body hanging outside**
  — the classic diagnostic posture in pulses. Boreholes on pods, absent seeds inside pods, and
  early-stage defoliation are the symptoms.
- **Pupae in the soil** — this is the key. **Deep summer ploughing exposes pupae to the sun, to
  desiccation and to birds**, and is the single most effective off-season action against
  *Helicoverpa*. It works because the vulnerable stage is in the soil, not on the plant.
(eagri ENTO 331 Lecture 06 — http://eagri.org/eagri50/ENTO331/lecture06/001.html)

### Off-season survival
Pupae in the soil enter a **facultative diapause** in cool or dry conditions, allowing the
population to bridge the gap between crops. In warm parts of India generations are continuous on a
sequence of hosts: cotton → pigeonpea/chickpea → tomato → chilli → sunflower. The pest's ability to
move across this crop sequence is why **area-wide, cropping-system-level thinking beats field-level
thinking**.

### Seasonal peak and weather
- On **chickpea and pigeonpea**: peak damage **December to February**, at the pod initiation and
  pod filling stage.
- On **cotton**: **August to October**.
- On **tomato and chilli**: through the fruiting phase, **October to March**.
- **Warm, dry weather after a rain event favours build-up**; the moths are strong migrants and
  arrive on wind fronts. Cloudy, humid weather favours the natural NPV epizootic that often
  collapses populations.

### ETL
- **Pulses (redgram/chickpea): 1 larva per 5 plants at the pod initiation stage.**
  (eagri ENTO 331 Lecture 06 — http://eagri.org/eagri50/ENTO331/lecture06/001.html)
- **Cotton: an action threshold of 1 egg per plant or 1 larva per plant.**
  (eagri ENTO 331 Lecture 11 — http://eagri.org/eagri50/ENTO331/lecture11/002.html)
- **Tomato: 1 egg or 1 larva per plant**, or **5 % fruit damage**. ⚠
- **Pheromone traps at 12 per hectare** for monitoring adult moth activity; **8–10 moths per trap
  per night for 3 consecutive nights** signals the start of oviposition. ⚠
(eagri ENTO 331 Lecture 06 and Lecture 11)

### Spread
Adults are **strong, long-distance migrants**, flying tens to hundreds of kilometres on wind
systems, which is why an outbreak can appear in a region with no local source. Larvae move only
short distances between adjacent plants.

### Natural enemies in India — named
- **Egg parasitoids**: *Trichogramma chilonis*, *T. pretiosum*, *Trichogrammatoidea bactrae*.
  Inundative release of *Trichogramma* spp. is the standard recommendation, timed to egg-laying
  detected by pheromone traps.
- **Egg-larval parasitoid**: **_Chelonus blackburnii_** — named explicitly in the TNAU package.
- **Larval parasitoids**: *Campoletis chlorideae* (the most important in Indian pulses),
  *Carcelia illota*, *Goniophthalmus halli*, *Eucelatoria bryani*, *Bracon (Habrobracon) hebetor*.
- **Pupal parasitoid**: *Tetrastichus* spp.
- **Predators**: *Chrysoperla zastrowi sillemi* (green lacewing), coccinellids *Coccinella
  septempunctata* and *Cheilomenes sexmaculata*, reduviid bugs *Rhynocoris* spp., the pentatomid
  *Eocanthecona furcellata*, ants, and **spiders**. **Birds** are major predators of larvae.
- **Entomopathogens**: ***Helicoverpa armigera* nucleopolyhedrovirus (HaNPV)** — a naturally
  occurring virus specific to *Helicoverpa*, applied in the evening because it is inactivated by
  ultraviolet light; *Nomuraea rileyi*, *Beauveria bassiana*, *Metarhizium anisopliae*, and
  *Bacillus thuringiensis* var. *kurstaki*.
- **Conservation measures explicitly recommended**:
  - **Bird perches at 50 per hectare** so that insectivorous birds pick larvae off the crop — free,
    highly effective, and specific to this pest's exposed feeding habit.
  - **Intercropping and border cropping** — pulses (cowpea, greengram, blackgram, soybean) with
    cotton raise spider and coccinellid activity; **marigold as a trap crop** in tomato is
    standard, because *Helicoverpa* prefers marigold flowers for oviposition, concentrating eggs
    where they can be destroyed.
  - **Sorghum or maize border rows** provide a perch and shelter for predators.
  - Need-based, ETL-driven intervention and avoidance of broad-spectrum synthetic pyrethroid use,
    which the TNAU package identifies as a driver of resurgence.
(eagri ENTO 331 Lecture 06 — http://eagri.org/eagri50/ENTO331/lecture06/001.html; Lecture 11 —
http://eagri.org/eagri50/ENTO331/lecture11/002.html)

### Resistance
*H. armigera* is one of the world's most resistance-prone insects: high fecundity, many
generations per year, polyphagy across an entire cropping system, and long-distance migration that
mixes selected populations across regions. The TNAU package states the principle directly:
**alternate different groups of insecticides for each round of spray; avoid tank-mix combinations;
apply only when the population crosses ETL**. Rotating modes of action ensures survivors of one
selection pressure meet a different one in the next generation.
(eagri ENTO 331 Lecture 11 — http://eagri.org/eagri50/ENTO331/lecture11/002.html)

### Virus transmission
None. *Helicoverpa* is a chewing pest and transmits no plant virus.
