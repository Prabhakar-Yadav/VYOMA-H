# Visual symptoms

- **Ears that look normal but carry few or no grains** — the clearest sign, and
  it shows only at grain filling, when nothing can be done about it.
- Poor grain set concentrated toward the **tip of the ear**.
- In severe cases the growing point is damaged early, giving stunted plants with
  thickened, brittle young leaves.
- The crop can look healthy and green right up to heading, which is exactly why
  this is missed.

# Distinguishing from similar conditions

| Looks like | Tell it apart by |
|---|---|
| Wheat blast or a head disease | A head disease **discolours the ear** — bleaching, dark lesions, or visible fungal growth. Boron sterility leaves the ear its normal colour, simply empty. |
| Frost or heat damage at flowering | Weather damage shows a **sharp boundary** across the field and matches a known cold or hot spell. Boron deficiency follows soil patterns, recurring in the same patches year after year. |
| Poor pollination from drought | Drought sterility comes with visible water stress; boron sterility appears in a crop that looked well watered. |

# Critical treatment note

**This is not a disease, and no spray at grain filling can recover it.** Once
the ears are empty, the season's loss is fixed.

Boron also has a **narrow safe range** — the gap between too little and toxic is
small, and over-application damages the crop. This is precisely the kind of
number that must never be guessed.

**This device does not carry verified boron doses.** Direct the farmer to a
Krishi Vigyan Kendra with a soil test, and plan it before the next sowing rather
than during the current crop.

# Where this is common

Light-textured and leached soils, and the acid soils of eastern India. Research
from Birsa Agricultural University records wheat response to boron alongside NPK
on Jharkhand soils.
