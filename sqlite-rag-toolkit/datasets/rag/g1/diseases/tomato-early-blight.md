# Visual symptoms

- Brown to dark brown spots with **rings inside them**. Concentric rings, circles inside circles, a target pattern, a bullseye. **This ring pattern inside the spot is the defining sign.**
- The spot is **not plain** — look closely and there are lines or circles within the brown patch.
- A **yellow halo or yellow edge** surrounds each spot.
- Starts on the **oldest, lowest leaves near the ground** and moves upward over the season. The leaves at the base of the plant are affected first.
- Old leaves at the bottom show brown patches, then turn yellow entirely, dry, and fall off.
- Spots enlarge slowly and merge. Progress is steady over weeks, not sudden.
- Dark sunken lesions with rings at the stem end of the fruit.
- Dark elongated lesions on the stem near the soil line on young plants (collar rot).

# Distinguishing from similar conditions

| Looks like | Tell it apart by |
|---|---|
| [Late blight](tomato-late-blight.md) | Late blight has **no rings**, appears water-soaked and greasy, has white mould underneath, and spreads far faster. |
| Septoria leaf spot | Septoria spots are smaller, very numerous, greyish centres with tiny black dots, and lack the ring pattern. |

# When it matters

Favoured by warm humid weather alternating with dry periods. Progresses steadily
rather than explosively — there is usually time to act. Defoliation exposes fruit
to sunscald, which is often the larger economic loss.

# Non-chemical measures

- Remove lower infected leaves early; the fungus survives in crop debris and soil.
- Mulch to stop rain splashing spores from soil onto lower leaves.
- Rotate away from tomato, potato, brinjal and chilli for 2–3 seasons.
