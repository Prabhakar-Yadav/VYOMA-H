# Visual symptoms

- Yellow to straw-coloured lesions beginning at the **leaf tip or along the margin**, spreading downward.
- Lesion edges are **wavy and irregular**, not straight.
- Affected leaves dry out and turn greyish-white, appearing scorched.
- Early morning: milky or opaque **bacterial ooze droplets** on fresh lesions, drying to a yellow crust.
- Kresek phase in seedlings: whole leaves wilt, roll, turn yellow-grey, and the plant may die.
- Usually appears after heavy rain, flooding, or strong wind that injures leaves.

# Distinguishing from similar conditions

| Looks like | Tell it apart by |
|---|---|
| [Rice blast](rice-blast.md) | Blast makes **spindle/diamond-shaped spots with grey centres and brown borders** in the leaf middle. Blight starts at tips and margins. |
| Leaf scald | Scald lesions have distinct zonate bands of light and dark; blight does not. |
| Nitrogen deficiency | Deficiency yellows the whole leaf uniformly from the tip with no wavy lesion edge and no ooze. |

# Critical treatment note

**This is a bacterial disease. Fungicides do not control it.** A farmer who
sprays a fungicide here loses both the money and the crop. If a fungicide is
suggested for this condition, that recommendation is wrong.

Chemical options are limited and only suppressive. Management is primarily
cultural and varietal.

# Non-chemical measures — these matter more than spray

- **Stop nitrogen top-dressing immediately.** Excess N sharply worsens spread.
- Drain the field and let it dry where the crop stage allows.
- Use resistant varieties in the next season — the only durable control.
- Avoid clipping seedling tips at transplanting; injuries are entry points.
- Do not let irrigation water flow from an infected field into a clean one.
