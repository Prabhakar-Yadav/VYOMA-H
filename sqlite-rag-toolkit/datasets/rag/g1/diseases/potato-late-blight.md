# Visual symptoms

- **Irregular water-soaked dark brown to black blotches** starting at the **leaf tips and margins**, surrounded by a **pale green or yellowish halo**.
- On the **underside** in a humid morning, a **fine white downy fuzzy ring at the edge of the blotch** — this is the key sign.
- Whole field can look **blackened and burnt** within days.
- A distinct **foul rotting smell** from the crop.
- Stems show dark brown-black greasy lesions.
- On tubers: **purplish-brown sunken patches on the skin**, and when cut open a **granular dry reddish-brown rot** spreading irregularly into the flesh.
- Spreads fastest at 10–20 °C with fog or high humidity.

# When it matters

**This is the highest-urgency condition in the entire bundle.** A crop can be
destroyed in **5 to 10 days**. In the Indian potato belt — UP, West Bengal, Bihar
and Punjab — a foggy humid spell is enough to trigger it.

Act on **first sighting**. There is no threshold to wait for.

# Treatment

**No verified treatment record has been added for potato yet.**

The molecules used on tomato late blight are related, but the label registration,
dose and pre-harvest interval differ by crop, and using a tomato label on potato
would be off-label. The device will identify this disease and then direct the
farmer to their Krishi Vigyan Kendra rather than quote a dose it cannot verify.

Adding a verified potato label record is the highest-value next addition to this
bundle, because this is the disease where speed matters most.

# Non-chemical measures

- Earth up ridges well so spores washing off leaves cannot reach the tubers.
- Do not irrigate in the evening; keep foliage dry overnight.
- Destroy volunteer potato plants and cull piles — they carry the pathogen over.
- Harvest only after the haulm has been removed and the skin has set, to avoid
  carrying infection into storage.
- Do not plant potato next to tomato. The same pathogen moves between them.
