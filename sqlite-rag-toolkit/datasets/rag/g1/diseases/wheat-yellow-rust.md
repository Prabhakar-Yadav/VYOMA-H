# Visual symptoms

- **Bright yellow to orange powdery pustules arranged in straight, narrow stripes** running lengthwise along the leaf, following the veins. The stripe pattern is definitive.
- Yellow powder rubs off onto fingers or clothing when the leaf is touched.
- Stripes appear first on lower leaves, then move up the canopy.
- Severely affected leaves dry from the tip and die early.
- Patches in the field, often starting at a focal point and spreading outward in a circle.
- Late in the season, pustules turn black as the fungus switches spore stage.

# Distinguishing from similar conditions

| Looks like | Tell it apart by |
|---|---|
| Brown/leaf rust | Brown rust pustules are **orange-brown, round, and scattered randomly** — not in stripes. |
| Black/stem rust | Stem rust produces dark reddish-brown elongated pustules mainly on **stems and sheaths**, and tears the leaf surface. |
| Nutrient deficiency | No powder, nothing rubs off. |

# When it matters

**High urgency in North India.** Favoured by cool (10–18 °C) humid weather.
Yield loss can reach 50–70% if it establishes before grain filling. Yellow rust
spreads by wind over long distances — an outbreak in a neighbouring district is
itself a reason to scout daily.

Act on **first appearance of pustules**. Do not wait for a threshold.

# Non-chemical measures

- Grow resistant varieties — the primary control. Variety recommendations change
  as the pathogen evolves; check the current season's district advisory.
- Avoid very late sowing and excess nitrogen.
- Report early outbreaks to the district agriculture office; rust surveillance is
  coordinated nationally and early reports protect neighbouring farms.
