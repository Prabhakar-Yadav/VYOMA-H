# Visual symptoms

- **Ragged, elongated holes** chewed through leaves — as if torn rather than cut cleanly.
- Small round "shot-hole" windows on young leaves where the caterpillar scraped one surface.
- **Moist sawdust-like frass (droppings) sitting in the whorl** — the single most reliable field sign.
- Larva itself in the whorl: pale to dark brown, with an **inverted white Y on the head capsule** and **four dark spots in a square on the second-last segment**.
- In later stages, feeding on tassels and boring into cobs from the tip.

# Distinguishing from similar conditions

| Looks like | Tell it apart by |
|---|---|
| Stem borer | Stem borer bores into the **stem** leaving a small round entry hole; armyworm feeds openly in the whorl leaving frass. |
| Hail or wind damage | Physical damage has no frass and no larvae, and tears follow no feeding pattern. |

# When it matters

Most damaging at the early whorl stage. Commonly cited action threshold is
**5–10% of plants showing fresh whorl damage**. Feeding is inside the whorl, so
spray must be **directed into the whorl** — a broadcast spray over the canopy
largely misses the target and wastes the chemical.

# Non-chemical measures

- Hand-pick and crush larvae in small holdings — effective at low infestation.
- Apply dry sand mixed with a little lime into the whorl of affected plants.
- Pheromone traps at 4–5 per acre for early detection of moth arrival.
- Early, uniform sowing across a village reduces the pest carrying over between staggered plantings.
