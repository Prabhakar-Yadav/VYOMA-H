**What IPM means**
IPM is the official crop protection strategy of the Directorate of Plant Protection, Quarantine and Storage and the National Institute of Plant Health Management.
IPM brings together several ways of managing a pest — cultural, physical, mechanical, biological, botanical and chemical.
Chemical pesticide is kept as the last step, used only when the other steps have been tried and the pest is still causing economic damage.

**The order to work in**

1. **Start with a healthy crop.**
   Choose a resistant or tolerant variety recommended for the district.
   Use certified, healthy seed and treat it before sowing.
   Sow at the recommended time and spacing.
   Keep the soil healthy with organic matter and balanced nutrition.

2. **Use cultural practices.**
   Rotate crops so a pest loses its host.
   Practise summer deep ploughing to expose resting stages of pests to the sun and to birds.
   Keep field bunds clean of weed hosts.
   Grow trap crops and border crops as recommended for the main crop.
   Grow flowering plants on the bund to feed natural enemies.
   Destroy crop stubble that harbours a pest carry-over.

3. **Watch the field every week — AESA.**
   The official method is Agro-Ecosystem Analysis. Walk the field weekly.
   Count the pests. Also count the defenders — spiders, ladybird beetles, dragonflies, wasps, predatory bugs.
   Look at the plant's own condition and its ability to recover.
   Note the weather, the soil moisture and the crop stage.
   Draw the field picture and decide as a group at the Farmer Field School.

4. **Use physical and mechanical steps.**
   Hand-pick and destroy egg masses, larvae and infested shoots.
   Install pheromone traps to monitor and mass-trap moths.
   Install light traps, yellow sticky traps and blue sticky traps as recommended.
   Put up bird perches so birds pick larvae.
   Rogue out virus-infected plants early.

5. **Use biological control.**
   Release bio-control agents such as Trichogramma, Chrysoperla and predatory mites where recommended.
   Use bio-pesticides — Trichoderma, Pseudomonas, Bacillus thuringiensis, Beauveria, Metarhizium, NPV.
   Use neem-based botanical formulations.
   Protect the natural enemies already in the field by holding back on broad-spectrum sprays.

6. **Use chemical pesticide last.**
   Spray only when the pest crosses the Economic Threshold Level for that crop and pest.
   Choose a pesticide approved by the Central Insecticides Board and Registration Committee for that crop and that pest.
   Choose the least hazardous product that will do the job.
   Rotate chemical groups across sprays so the pest does not build resistance.
   Spot-spray the affected patch instead of the whole field where the pest is localised.

**Key idea to remember**
A pest present in the field is not by itself a reason to spray.
Spraying is justified when the pest population crosses the threshold at which the damage costs more than the control.
Every spray that goes out before that point also kills the defenders that were working for free.
