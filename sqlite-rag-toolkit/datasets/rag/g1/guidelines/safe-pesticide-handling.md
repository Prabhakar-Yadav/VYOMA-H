Source of this guidance: Directorate of Plant Protection, Quarantine and Storage, "Instruction for Safe Use of Pesticide".

### Buying

Do:
- Buy from a licensed, registered pesticide dealer.
- Ask for a proper cash memo with the product name, batch number and date.
- Check that the container is sealed and the label is intact and readable.
- Check the expiry date on the label.
- Buy only the quantity needed for the immediate operation.
- Check that the product is approved for that crop and that pest.

The safest habit: read the whole label before buying, and buy for the job at hand.

### Storing

Do:
- Keep pesticides in a separate store away from the house, away from the kitchen and away from the cattle shed.
- Keep the product in its own original container with the label on it.
- Keep the store locked, with children and animals well away.
- Keep weedicides on a separate shelf from insecticides and fungicides.
- Put a warning sign on the store.
- Protect stock from direct sunlight and from rain.

Keeping the product in its original labelled container is the single most important storage rule. It is what tells a doctor what to treat, if anything goes wrong.

### Mixing the spray solution

Do:
- Wear full protective gear — hand gloves, face mask, cap, apron or full-sleeve cotton clothing, full trousers and covered footwear.
- Use clean water.
- Read the label and measure the recommended quantity with a separate marked measure.
- Mix in the open air, standing so the wind carries the fumes away.
- Use a stick to stir, and keep hands out of the solution.
- Prepare only the quantity needed for that day and use it the same day.
- Keep drinking water, soap and a change of clothes nearby.

Keep the mouth, eyes and skin clear of the concentrate. Keep food, drink, tobacco and bidis outside the mixing area.

### Spraying

Do:
- Spray on a calm, cool day. Early morning and late afternoon are the good windows.
- Stand so the wind carries the spray away from the body, and walk backwards out of the sprayed area.
- Check the sprayer for leaks before filling it.
- Clear the nozzle with a soft brush or by washing it, keeping it away from the mouth.
- Put up a warning sign on a freshly sprayed field.
- Take a break and step into fresh air if the head feels heavy.
- Keep other people, children and grazing animals away from the field during and just after spraying.

Choose a calm day, keep the wind at the back, and stay out of the drift.

### After spraying

Do:
- Wash the sprayer, the nozzles and the measuring vessels in an isolated spot away from wells, ponds, canals and drinking water sources.
- Pour leftover spray solution into a pit dug in an isolated waste area, well away from any water body.
- Take a full bath with soap and put on clean clothes.
- Wash the sprayed clothes separately from family clothes.
- Wash hands and face thoroughly before eating, drinking or smoking.
- Note the date of spraying in a field diary, so the pre-harvest interval can be counted.

### Empty container disposal

Official practice:
1. Rinse the empty container three times with clean water. Pour each rinse into the spray tank and use it on the crop.
2. Puncture and crush the empty container so it can never be used again.
3. Bury the crushed container in a deep pit dug in a waste area, away from any water source and away from grazing land.
4. Keep the empty container out of the house entirely.

An empty pesticide container is treated as unusable for anything else. It is not used for storing water, grain, oil, milk or feed, and it is not sold to a scrap dealer for reuse.
