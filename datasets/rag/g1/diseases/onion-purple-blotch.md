# Visual symptoms

- Starts as **small white sunken specks with a purple centre** on leaves and seed stalks.
- These enlarge into **elongated spindle-shaped lesions with a clearly PURPLE-BROWN centre showing concentric rings**.
- Each lesion is bordered by a **yellow zone that extends well above and below** the lesion itself.
- Lesions **girdle the leaf**, and the leaf **topples over or breaks** at the lesion point.
- Bulbs rot at the neck — semi-watery and yellow at first, darkening to a deep red rot.

# Distinguishing from similar conditions

| Looks like | Tell it apart by |
|---|---|
| Stemphylium blight | Stemphylium is **olive-brown to almost black** rather than purple, starts as flecks in the **middle of the leaf blade** rather than working from a speck, and leaves a dark velvety layer on dead tissue. |
| Onion thrips damage | Thrips gives **silvery-white streaks with tiny black faecal dots** and no purple lesion. |

**Note:** purple blotch and Stemphylium blight very often occur together and are
genuinely hard to separate in a photograph. Where the two are both plausible the
device should abstain rather than pick.

# Treatment

**No verified treatment record has been added for onion yet.** The device will
identify the condition and direct the farmer to their Krishi Vigyan Kendra.

# Non-chemical measures

- Avoid overhead irrigation; long leaf wetness drives infection.
- Wider spacing and good airflow between rows.
- Rotate away from onion and garlic for 2–3 seasons.
- Remove and destroy infected debris after harvest rather than leaving it in the field.
- Cure bulbs properly before storage; neck rot follows field infection into store.
