# Visual symptoms

**Shoot stage:**

- **Young growing shoot tips wilt, droop and dry** while the rest of the plant stays green and healthy. This contrast is the giveaway.
- The wilted tip **breaks off easily and is hollow inside**.

**Fruit stage:**

- A **round bore hole on the fruit surface, plugged with dried brown excreta (frass)**.
- Cutting the fruit open reveals **dirty frass-filled tunnels** with a **pinkish-white caterpillar** inside.
- Damaged fruit is unsaleable even when the outside looks nearly fine.

# When it matters

The largest single cause of brinjal loss in India — commonly **40 to 70 percent
of fruit damaged** where unmanaged. Because the caterpillar feeds **inside** the
shoot or fruit, sprays applied after it has bored in do not reach it. Timing and
mechanical removal matter more than chemistry.

# Treatment

**No verified treatment record has been added for brinjal yet.** The device will
identify the pest and direct the farmer to their Krishi Vigyan Kendra.

# Non-chemical measures

These are unusually effective for this pest and should be emphasised:

- **Clip off and destroy wilted shoots** twice a week, cutting below the bored
  portion. This removes the caterpillar before it reaches the fruiting stage and
  is the single most effective step a smallholder can take.
- Remove and destroy bored fruits at every picking. Do not leave them on field bunds.
- Pheromone traps at 8–10 per acre to track moth activity and time any action.
- Do not grow brinjal continuously on the same plot; rotate away for a season.
- Remove and burn crop residue after the final harvest.
