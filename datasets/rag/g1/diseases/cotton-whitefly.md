# Visual symptoms

Retrieval text — written to match what a vision model reports from a photograph.

- Tiny white winged insects on the **underside** of leaves; they fly up in a cloud when the plant is disturbed.
- Leaves turn **yellow**, starting between the veins, then curl **downward**.
- Sticky, shiny coating (honeydew) on the upper surface of lower leaves.
- **Black sooty mould** growing on the sticky coating — looks like soot or ash smeared on the leaf.
- Ants often present, feeding on the honeydew.
- In severe cases: stunted plants, premature leaf drop, small or unopened bolls.

# Distinguishing from similar conditions

| Looks like | Tell it apart by |
|---|---|
| [Cotton jassid](jassid.md) | Jassid causes leaf margins to yellow then turn **red-brown and cup upward**; whitefly curls downward. |
| Nutrient deficiency | Deficiency yellowing is uniform and has **no insects and no sticky coating**. |
| [Cotton leaf curl virus](cotton-leaf-curl-virus.md) | Virus causes thickened veins and upward cupping; whitefly is the *vector*, so both may be present together. |

# When it matters

Peak incidence in the warm dry period after monsoon withdrawal. Economic threshold
is commonly cited as **6–8 adults per leaf** on the top three leaves. Below this,
spraying is discouraged — it kills natural predators and accelerates resistance.

# Non-chemical measures

- Yellow sticky traps at 8–10 per acre for monitoring and mass trapping.
- Avoid excess nitrogen — lush growth increases whitefly buildup.
- Conserve natural enemies (*Encarsia*, *Chrysoperla*); avoid repeat pyrethroid sprays.
