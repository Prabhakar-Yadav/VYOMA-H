### When to take a sample
Take the sample after harvest, when the field carries no standing crop.
The usual times are after the Rabi harvest and after the Kharif harvest.
Take it before the next crop's fertiliser and manure go in.
Test the same field once every 2 to 3 years, or every year for intensively cropped and irrigated land.

### How to draw the sample

1. **Divide the farm into uniform blocks.**
   Treat each block separately where the soil colour, slope, past cropping or irrigation differs.
   One composite sample represents one uniform block.

2. **Choose 8 to 10 spots** spread across the block, walking a zigzag path.

3. **Clear the surface** at each spot. Scrape away grass, stubble, pebbles and fresh manure from the top.

4. **Cut a V-shaped slice.**
   Dig a V-shaped pit to the required depth. Then shave a uniform 2 to 3 cm thick slice from top to bottom of one face of the V.
   Depth for field crops: 0 to 15 cm, the plough layer.
   Depth for orchards and plantation crops: take separate samples for 0 to 30 cm, 30 to 60 cm and 60 to 90 cm.

5. **Collect all slices in one clean bucket** or on a clean cloth or polythene sheet.

6. **Mix thoroughly.**

7. **Reduce by quartering.**
   Spread the mixed soil into a circle. Divide it into four quarters. Throw away two opposite quarters. Mix the remaining two. Repeat until about half a kilogram of soil is left.

8. **Bag and label.**
   Put the soil in a clean cloth or polythene bag.
   Write on the label: farmer's name, village, block, district, survey or khasra number, field identification, sampling depth, date, the previous crop grown, the next crop planned, and whether the land is irrigated or rainfed.
   Put one label inside the bag and tie one outside.

### Where to avoid drawing the sample
Draw the sample away from field bunds and boundaries, away from the old manure heap site, away from trees and shade, away from the irrigation channel, away from marshy and waterlogged spots, away from where the compost or fertiliser was last dumped, and away from the cattle standing area. These spots do not represent the field.

### Where to send it
- The village level soil testing lab, where the state has set one up
- The block or district Soil Testing Laboratory of the state agriculture department
- The nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra
- The State Agricultural University soil science laboratory
- A mobile soil testing van when it visits the village

Register the sample on soilhealth.dac.gov.in, or let the lab register it. Keep the sample registration number and the mobile number handy to collect the report.

### How to read a Soil Health Card

The card lists 12 values with a rating for each.

**pH — how acid or alkaline the soil is.**
Around 6.5 to 7.5 is the comfortable range for most crops.
A value below about 6.0 means acidic soil. Liming is the usual correction.
A value above about 8.0 means alkaline or sodic soil. Gypsum and organic matter are the usual correction.
pH matters because it decides how much of the nutrient present in the soil the plant can actually take up.

**EC — Electrical Conductivity, the salt level.**
A low value means normal soil. A high value means salt is building up and will stress the crop.

**OC — Organic Carbon, the life of the soil.**
Higher is better. Low organic carbon means the soil needs farmyard manure, compost, green manure and crop residue incorporation.
Organic carbon drives water holding, structure and nutrient release.

**N, P, K — the main nutrients.**
Each is rated Low, Medium or High.
Low means the crop will need the full recommended dose or more.
Medium means the standard recommended dose.
High means the dose can be reduced for that nutrient.

**S — Sulphur.** Important for oilseeds, pulses and mustard.

**Zn, Fe, Cu, Mn, B — micronutrients.**
Each is rated Sufficient or Deficient.
A deficient rating calls for a specific micronutrient application, often as a soil dose at sowing or a foliar spray at the recommended crop stage.

**The recommendation block**
The card carries a crop-wise fertiliser recommendation table.
It gives the quantity of fertiliser per acre or per hectare for the crops the farmer named.
It usually gives two options — a straight fertiliser combination and an option including organic manure.

**What to do with the card**
1. Take the card to the fertiliser shop and buy to the recommendation, not to habit.
2. Correct pH and organic carbon first. Nutrient response improves once those are right.
3. Apply the micronutrient the card marks as deficient. This is often where the biggest yield gain sits.
4. Show the card to the KVK scientist or the agriculture officer if any rating is unclear.
5. Keep the card and compare the next test in 2 to 3 years, to see whether organic carbon is climbing.
