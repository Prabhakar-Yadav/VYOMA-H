**What a KVK is**
A Krishi Vigyan Kendra is the district level farm science centre of the Indian Council of Agricultural Research.
There are 731 KVKs covering 638 rural districts. The first one was set up in Puducherry in 1974.
A KVK is hosted by a State Agricultural University, an ICAR institute, a state department or a registered NGO, and staffed by subject matter specialists in agronomy, plant protection, horticulture, soil science, animal science, home science and agricultural engineering.

**What a KVK does for the farmer**
- Diagnoses crop problems. Bring an affected plant, and get an identification and an advisory.
- Runs a soil, water and plant testing service.
- Runs on-farm demonstrations of new varieties and practices, on farmers' own fields.
- Runs skill training — nursery raising, grafting, seed production, mushroom growing, beekeeping, vermicompost, dairy management, food processing, farm machinery operation.
- Produces and supplies quality seed, planting material, bio-fertiliser and bio-agent cultures.
- Issues local agro-advisories on weather, sowing windows and pest outbreaks, including through the Kisan Sarathi platform.
- Runs Farmer Field Schools and field days.
- Guides farmers into FPOs and into value addition and marketing.

**Good reasons to visit a KVK**
- A crop problem the farmer cannot identify — bring a whole affected plant with roots, and photos of the field.
- Choosing a variety for the coming season.
- Getting soil tested and getting the recommendation explained.
- Learning a new enterprise — mushroom, apiary, poultry, goat rearing, vermicompost, processing.
- Getting a fair second opinion on a dealer's spray recommendation.
- Starting organic or natural farming.
- Understanding which schemes are running in the district this year.
- Getting genuine seed and planting material of a new variety.

**How to find and approach a KVK**
1. Find the district KVK on kvk.icar.gov.in. The portal lists every KVK with address, phone number and staff.
2. Phone ahead and ask which day the subject specialist is available. Many KVKs keep a fixed farmers' day each week.
3. Carry the affected plant sample, a soil sample if a test is wanted, the land record and Aadhaar.
4. Register on Kisan Sarathi (kisansarathi.icar.gov.in) to receive advisories on the mobile.
5. Ask to be added to the KVK's farmer group so training announcements reach the phone.

Most KVK advisory and diagnostic services are free. Seed, planting material and some trainings carry a small cost.
