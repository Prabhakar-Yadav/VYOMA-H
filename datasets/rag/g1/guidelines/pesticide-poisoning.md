**Signs to act on**
Headache, dizziness, heavy sweating, excess saliva, watering eyes, blurred vision, pinpoint pupils, nausea, vomiting, stomach cramps, muscle twitching, weakness, difficulty breathing, confusion, fits.
Any of these appearing during or after handling pesticide calls for immediate action.

**Steps, in order**

1. **Move the person to fresh air, away from the pesticide.**
   Get the person out of the sprayed field or the store immediately.

2. **Remove contaminated clothing.**
   Take off all clothes that have pesticide on them. Wrap the person in something clean.

3. **Wash the skin.**
   Wash the affected skin, hair and nails with plenty of clean water and soap. Keep washing for at least 15 minutes.

4. **Rinse the eyes if the eyes are affected.**
   Hold the eyelids open and rinse with clean running water for 15 minutes.

5. **Keep the person calm, resting and warm.**
   Loosen tight clothing at the neck and waist. Turn an unconscious person on their side so the airway stays clear.

6. **Call for help and get the person to a hospital.**
   Call the ambulance on **108**.
   Call the National Poisons Information Centre at AIIMS New Delhi, toll free, 24 hours a day: **1800 116 117**.

7. **Carry the pesticide container or the label to the hospital.**
   This is the most useful thing anyone can do. The doctor needs to know the exact chemical to choose the right antidote.
   Carry the empty container, or the label, or a clear photo of the label.

8. **Tell the doctor everything.**
   Say what was used, how much, how long ago, and by what route — skin, breathing or swallowing.

**Things that help**
- Keep the person awake and lying on their side.
- Keep the airway clear.
- Carry the label.

**Things to leave to the doctor**
- Giving milk, oil or any home remedy by mouth.
- Making the person vomit.
- Giving anything by mouth to a person who is drowsy, unconscious or having fits.

The National Poisons Information Centre runs 24 hours from the Department of Pharmacology, AIIMS New Delhi, and answers calls from the public and from doctors.
