# Which yellowing is it

A yellow crop is one of the most common things a farmer reports, and it is the
single easiest symptom to misread. Three questions separate almost all cases.

**1. Which leaves went yellow first, top or bottom?**

| Answer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bottom, older leaves** | Nitrogen. It moves inside the plant, so the crop pulls it out of old leaves to feed new growth. |
| **Top, younger leaves** | Iron or sulphur. Neither moves once placed, so shortage shows in whatever grows next. |

**2. Is the whole leaf yellow, or only between the veins?**

| Answer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Whole leaf, evenly | Nitrogen (bottom) or sulphur (top) |
| **Veins stay green, tissue between them yellows** | **Iron** — this net-like pattern is close to conclusive |

**3. Is it spreading plant to plant?**

Deficiency does not spread. It sits where the soil is short, in the same patches
each season. A yellowing that advances outward over days, or moves along a row,
is a disease or an insect vector — not hunger.

# Nitrogen deficiency

- Even pale green to yellow, starting with the **oldest bottom leaves**.
- Yellowing often runs from the leaf tip along the midrib in a V shape in cereals.
- Whole plant stunted, few tillers, thin stems.
- Worst after heavy rain on light soils, where nitrogen leaches away.

# Iron deficiency

- **Young top leaves** yellow while **veins stay distinctly green**.
- In severe cases the new leaves come out almost white.
- Common on alkaline and calcareous soils, and in waterlogged patches.
- Note the reversal worth remembering: iron shortage is a problem of **alkaline**
  soils, whereas zinc shortage is common on **acid** soils. Over-liming an acid
  soil can create iron deficiency where none existed.

# Distinguishing from disease

| Looks like | Tell it apart by |
|---|---|
| Yellow mosaic virus | Virus gives **mottled patches of yellow and green mixed on the same leaf**, often with puckering, and spreads plant to plant. Deficiency is even and static. |
| Wheat yellow rust | Rust gives **yellow-orange powdery stripes** that leave coloured dust on a finger. Deficiency has no powder and no stripe. |
| Bacterial leaf blight | Blight makes wavy-edged lesions drying straw-white from the leaf tip. Deficiency has no lesion edge at all. |

# Critical treatment note

**None of these is a disease.** A fungicide sprayed on a hungry crop costs money
and fixes nothing.

**This device does not carry verified fertiliser doses.** Nitrogen rate depends
on the crop, the stage, the soil and what has already been applied — and excess
nitrogen carries its own harms, including greater disease susceptibility and
lodging. Direct the farmer to a Krishi Vigyan Kendra or a soil test.
