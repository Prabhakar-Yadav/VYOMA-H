# Visual symptoms

- **Dusty brown to rust-coloured spots** on the lower leaves, appearing on a
  leaf that has already gone slightly pale.
- **Bronzing from the leaf margin inward**, while the midrib stays green
  noticeably longer than the rest of the blade.
- Whole blade eventually turns bronze to brown in prolonged cases.
- **Stunted, uneven growth across the field** — patches of short plants beside
  normal ones, rather than a spreading front.
- **Tillering reduced or stopped.**
- Appears **2 to 3 weeks after transplanting**, and often worst in the parts of
  a field that stay wettest.

# Distinguishing from similar conditions

This is the section that matters. Zinc deficiency is regularly sprayed with
fungicide because it looks like disease at a glance.

| Looks like | Tell it apart by |
|---|---|
| [Rice bacterial leaf blight](../diseases/rice-bacterial-leaf-blight.md) | Blight starts at the **leaf tip or margin as a wavy-edged lesion** that dries straw-white, and shows bacterial ooze in the morning. Zinc shows scattered **spots on a pale leaf** plus stunting, no ooze, no wavy lesion edge. |
| Rice blast | Blast makes **spindle or diamond-shaped spots with grey centres** and clear brown borders. Zinc spots are irregular and dusty, with no defined centre. |
| Brown spot (fungal) | Brown spot lesions are **oval with a distinct dark border and grey centre**, evenly scattered on green tissue. Zinc spotting sits on tissue that is already discoloured, with bronzing at the margins. |
| Iron deficiency | Iron yellows the **young** leaves between the veins; zinc affects **older** leaves first with brown spotting and bronzing. |

**The decisive test is spread.** A disease spreads — it moves outward from
focal points, and neighbouring plants catch it over days. A deficiency does
not spread; it appears where the soil is short of the nutrient and stays put.
If patches have looked the same for a fortnight without advancing, suspect
nutrition, not infection.

# Critical treatment note

**This is not a disease. No fungicide, bactericide or insecticide can fix it.**

A farmer who sprays here spends money, loses the crop anyway, and puts
pesticide into the field for nothing. If any product is suggested for these
symptoms, that recommendation is wrong.

The correction is a zinc application, and the right amount depends on the
soil's own zinc status, which needs a soil test. **This device does not carry
verified zinc doses.** The farmer should be directed to a Krishi Vigyan Kendra
or soil testing laboratory.

# Where this is common

Widespread in eastern India on acid alfisols and red lateritic soils, and in
continuously flooded rice. Research from Birsa Agricultural University, Ranchi
documents zinc response in rice on Jharkhand's acid alfisols specifically.
Prolonged flooding lowers zinc availability, which is why the wettest parts of
a field often show it first.
